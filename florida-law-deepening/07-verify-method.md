# HOW TO VERIFY A LEGAL CLAIM

**Purpose:** This method exists because of Part 2. On Nov 23, 2026, Muse3 stated 17 wrong or unsupported legal claims to Git-FED in a single day — wrong fine totals, a wrong deadline, stale agency numbers, wrong statute citations, and worker figures relayed as fact without checking them. Every rule below maps to a specific error from that day. Follow the method and those errors do not repeat.

**The one-sentence version:** Open the current statute text yourself, check it against the paper the clerk printed, and never state a number you have not seen in one of those two places.

---

## 1. The primary-source hierarchy

Sources rank in this order. A higher source beats a lower one every time they disagree. A lower source never overrules a higher one, no matter how confident it sounds.

### Level 1 — The statute or rule text on Online Sunshine (leg.state.fl.us)

The current Florida Statutes and Florida Rules of Court, read live on the official site. This is the controlling source. Everything else is checked against it.

**What beats what, with the day's example:** The lifetime traffic-school limit. FLHSMV's BDI page and Broward's affidavit form both say 5 elections in a lifetime. A 2024 statute-text read said 8 (§ 318.14(9): "may not make more than eight elections within his or her lifetime"). The statute is Level 1 and the agency pages are Level 3, so the statute wins — **but** the later FLHSMV study found the current FLHSMV pages expressly attribute 5 to § 318.14(9) effective July 1, 2024, and the "8" now survives only in a legacy FAQ. That means the limit is currently **unresolved**: do not assert 5 or 8 as settled until the current § 318.14(9) text is opened live on Online Sunshine and quoted. The lesson is not "the statute said 8, case closed." The lesson is that a statute read in an earlier sweep is not the same as the current statute text opened now. Level 1 means the live text, read today.

### Level 2 — The clerk's printed figure / the court record

The paper citation, the clerk's case-search result, the clerk's printed notice. For anything case-specific — the amount due, the deadline printed on the ticket, whether a filing exists — this beats statute arithmetic and beats every website.

**What beats what, with the day's example:** The $129 vs. $166 fine totals. Neither number appears as a stated total anywhere in § 318.18. Both are arithmetically reachable after county add-ons ($129 = $95 + $34; $166 = $95 + $71), which is exactly why both got asserted — $166 was even declared to "check out." Arithmetic is not verification. The statute sets components, not a total. The only total that counts is the one the clerk printed on the citation or the clerk's case record. If the clerk's figure and your arithmetic disagree, the clerk's figure wins and your arithmetic is wrong somewhere.

### Level 3 — The agency page (FLHSMV, clerk site, E-Filing Portal FAQ)

Authoritative on the procedure and fees that agency itself administers — but agencies go stale, and a stale agency page is the single most common error source in this file. Treat every agency page as current-until-proven-stale, and prove it current before relying on it (see section 2).

**What beats what, with the day's example:** FLHSMV Appendix C (revised April 2, 2025) confirms § 316.238 failure to dim is a moving infraction, 3 points, $60 base fine. OTR's case-page label for the same ticket said "non-moving, 0 points, up to $129" — a platform page, not an agency page, and it matched § 316.237 equipment, not § 316.238. The agency table beat the platform label. But note the limit: the Broward Clerk's own affidavit form (Rev. 05/12/2026) cites § 28.24(8) for the $7 driving-school fee, and § 28.24(8) is $6.00 for copying per page — the $7 charge is actually § 28.24(9)(a)-(b). A clerk form can be wrong on the citation while the amount is right. Level 3 never beats Level 1 on a citation.

### Level 4 — Secondary sources (law-firm blogs, AI answers, platform pages, worker reports)

Never a source. At most a lead that tells you what to check at Levels 1–3. A worker's report is Level 4, not Level 1 — relaying it as fact is how the day's errors spread.

**What beats what, with the day's example:** One sweep worker reported the July 1, 2024 amendment backwards, claiming the change cut the lifetime limit to 5. Another worker's figure ($166) was relayed to Git-FED as "checks out." Both were passed through unverified. The rule that would have stopped both: a worker's word is a claim, not evidence. Quote the line from the statute or the clerk's page, or say "not verified."

**The hierarchy in one table:**

| Level | Source | Controls | Can be overruled by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Statute / rule text, Online Sunshine, read live | What the law says | Nothing |
| 2 | Clerk's printed figure / court record | Amount due, ticket deadline, whether a filing exists | Nothing on case-specific facts |
| 3 | Agency page (FLHSMV, clerk site) | Procedure and fees that agency administers | Levels 1–2 |
| 4 | Blogs, AI answers, platform pages, worker reports | Nothing — leads only | Everything above |

## 2. How to spot a stale agency page

Agency pages rot quietly. They stay live, keep ranking in search, and keep stating numbers the law no longer supports. Four checks, in order:

**a. Read the page's own date line first.** Look for "effective," "revised," "last updated," or a form revision date before reading any number on the page. If there is no date line, treat the page as undated and therefore untrusted on any number that could have changed. Examples from this file: FLHSMV's 2026 legislation page lists years only through 2022 and was last updated around 2021 — a live page four years stale. Broward's driving-school affidavit form is Rev. 05/12/2026 and still says 5 lifetime elections and still cites § 28.24(8) for the $7 fee. A recent revision date does not prove the content is current; it only proves the form was reprinted.

**b. Cross-check the number against the current statute text.** Open § 318.14(9) (or the governing section) live on Online Sunshine and compare, number for number. If the agency page and the statute disagree, the statute wins and the page is flagged stale in the file — do not silently "fix" the page's number in your head and move on. Record both numbers and which one controls.

**c. Watch for legacy FAQ pages vs. current pages on the same site.** FLHSMV keeps old FAQ pages live alongside current ones, and search results surface both. The legacy Driver Improvement Courses FAQ (dicfaqa.html) still says 8 lifetime elections; the current BDI page, Traffic Citations page, Driving Records FAQ, and Driver Improvement Schools page all say 5. A search-indexed copy of a page is not the page — during the Nov 23 sweep, many direct page opens hit HTTP 429 rate limits and facts had to rest on search-indexed copies of the same URLs, flagged as such in the file. A search-indexed copy can be an old crawl of a page that has since changed. If you could not open the live page, say so; do not present the indexed copy as a live read.

**d. Watch for the same site contradicting itself.** FLHSMV's General Information page says a vision result below 20/40 triggers an eye-specialist referral; its Vision Standards page puts the Form 72010 trigger at 20/50 or worse. Same agency, same topic, two different numbers. When a site contradicts itself, neither page is usable until the statute or rule behind it is opened.

### Case study: the FLHSMV 5-vs-8 traffic-school limit

The full sequence, because it shows every failure mode at once:

1. The statute was amended effective July 1, 2024, raising the lifetime limit from 5 to 8. A 2024 statute-text read confirmed "eight" in § 318.14(9), and the file resolved the limit at 8.
2. One sweep worker reported the amendment backwards, claiming the 2024 change cut the limit to 5. That report was Level 4 and should never have entered the file as a competing fact.
3. A later 18-worker FLHSMV site study found the **current** FLHSMV pages say 5 — BDI page, Traffic Citations page, Driving Records FAQ, Driver Improvement Schools page — and the Driving Records FAQ expressly attributes 5 to § 318.14(9), effective July 1, 2024. The Palm Beach Clerk still says 8. The "8" now survives only in the legacy FAQ (dicfaqa.html) and a stale search-indexed copy of the BDI page.
4. Status: **unresolved.** The hierarchy says the statute controls, but the live statute text has not been re-opened and quoted since the contradiction surfaced. The 2024 read may predate how the amendment was applied. Until the current § 318.14(9) is opened live on leg.state.fl.us and quoted verbatim, assert neither 5 nor 8.

For Git-FED personally the dispute changes nothing — he has 1 election on file and is eligible under either limit — which is exactly why it must be recorded as unresolved rather than quietly "resolved" at whichever number is convenient. A limit that does not affect him today will affect someone reading this file later.

## 3. The fine-total rule

**No fine total is ever asserted without one of these two things:**

1. **The clerk's printed figure** — the total on the paper citation, or the total in the clerk's online case search for that citation. This is the amount due. Full stop.
2. **The statute's base fine plus the costs statute, shown as components** — base fine from § 318.18, court costs and add-ons from § 318.18(12) and (14), late fee from § 318.18(8)(a) if applicable — with the total explicitly labeled as computed, not as the amount due.

If you have neither, you do not state a total. You state the base fine and the components, and you say the total is whatever the clerk printed.

### Worked example: § 316.238 failure to dim

- **Base fine: $60.** § 318.18(3)(a): "$60 for all moving violations not requiring a mandatory appearance."
- **Court costs: $35** for moving traffic infractions. § 318.18(12)(a)3 (older versions: (11)(a)3 — check which subsection number is current when you cite it). $60 + $35 = **$95 before other add-ons.**
- **Other add-ons in the same section:** § 318.18(12)(c) adds $2.50; § 318.18(12)(d) adds $3 plus $2 "when assessed by a municipality or county"; § 318.18(14)(a) allows a county surcharge of up to $30 for court facilities. Totals vary by county.
- **Late fee, if late: $16.** § 318.18(8)(a): failure to comply or pay within the 30-day period in § 318.14 adds $16.
- **If traffic school is elected: 18% reduction** of the civil penalty under § 318.18(3). § 318.14(9). The reduction applies to the civil penalty, not necessarily every component of the total due.
- **Total: whatever the clerk printed.** $129 ($95 + $34 in add-ons) and $166 ($95 + $71) are both arithmetically reachable — and neither was ever verified as the clerk's total for this ticket. That is the whole point: reachable is not verified. State $60 base + $35 costs + county add-ons, then give the clerk's printed total, or say the clerk's total has not been checked.

**The errors this rule prevents, from Part 2:** "$166 checks out" / "$166 total fine" (item 2); "up to $129 in fines" attached to the wrong statute (item 1); and the $8-record error (item 9), where a correct price ($8 for a 3-year record) was attached to a false requirement (no record purchase is required to elect traffic school, and the 3-year record does not even show school elections — the $10 complete record does). A correct number in the wrong role is still a wrong claim.

## 4. Deadline verification

**"30 days" is never computed from memory. It is computed from the citation date, on paper, every time.**

### The counting method — Fla. R. Gen. Prac. & Jud. Admin. 2.514(a)(1)

- **(A)** Begin counting from the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday.
- **(B)** Count every day, including intermediate Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays.
- **(C)** If the last day is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the period runs until the end of the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday.
- **Scope caveat:** Rule 2.514(a) applies only to a period in a rule, local rule, court order, or statute "that does not specify a method of computing time." If the governing traffic statute or traffic-court rule specifies its own counting, that controls. Broward Clerk says civil traffic infractions must be paid or an option elected within **30 calendar days** from the citation date.

### Worked example: Git-FED's ticket, issued Sat Oct 24, 2026

- **Under Rule 2.514:** trigger Sat Oct 24 → next non-weekend day is Mon Oct 26 = day 1 → **day 30 = Tue Nov 24, 2026.**
- **Under simple calendar counting** (exclude Oct 24, count every day): day 1 is Sun Oct 25 → **day 30 = Mon Nov 23, 2026.**
- **Nov 22 is wrong under every version.** It counts the trigger day itself as day 1 and lands on a Sunday, which the rule rolls forward. Muse3's "window closed Nov 22" (Part 2, item 3) was the include-the-trigger-day error.
- **Safe course: treat Mon Nov 23 as the deadline.** Veterans' Day, Wed Nov 11, 2026, is a § 110.117 holiday but intermediate, so it counts under (a)(1)(B) and does not move the date. Thanksgiving (Thu Nov 26) and the Friday after (Nov 27) fall after the deadline.
- **The +5 days in Rule 2.514(b) does not apply.** It applies only after service by mail or email, not to a citation issued in person.

### Why the paper citation controls over memory

The ticket itself says "must comply within 30 days." That printed line, plus the clerk's "30 calendar days from the citation date," is the deadline that governs the case — not a remembered date, not a date computed in chat and never re-checked. Before stating any deadline: (1) read the date on the paper citation; (2) compute it both ways above; (3) state the earlier date as the safe deadline; (4) name the trigger date in the same sentence so the computation can be re-checked.

### Related deadline errors from Part 2

- **"D6 suspension hits at midnight after the deadline" — false (item 7).** § 318.15(1)(a): the clerk has up to 10 days to notify FLHSMV, then FLHSMV issues an order effective 20 days after the order is provided under § 322.251(1), (2), and (6). No suspension is effective at midnight on day 31 — roughly 30 days out from the deadline. A deadline and a suspension effective date are different dates; never state one as the other.
- **"She legally has to accept or reject today" — overstated (item 5).** OTR gives the attorney one full business day; that is a platform term ("we may reassign"), not a legal duty, and it allows counter-offers or questions instead of a straight accept/decline. A platform's business-day window is not a statute's deadline. Name the source of every deadline: statute, rule, court order, or platform term — and never upgrade a platform term into a legal duty.
- **"You're covered because you hired her Nov 21" — false (item 4).** No attorney-client relationship exists until the attorney accepts. Booking places only a pre-authorization hold. Nov 21, 2026 was a Saturday; the case was only "pending review." A status label on a platform dashboard ("pending review") is not a legal fact about representation.
- **The 180-day hearing window** (§ 318.15(1)(c)): a hearing on the underlying infraction may be requested within 180 days after the violation date. For the Oct 24, 2026 ticket: **Thu April 22, 2027**. Compute it the same way — from the violation date, on paper — and note it is a hearing on the infraction, not on the suspension.

## 5. Checklist — run before asserting any legal fact to Git-FED

1. **Did I open the current statute or rule text live on Online Sunshine, or am I relying on a sweep read, a search-indexed copy, or memory?** If not live, say "not verified" — do not state it as fact.
2. **Does the citation check out?** Open the section and subsection named. The day's wrong citations: § 28.24(8) for the $7 fee (it is § 28.24(9)(a)-(b); (8) is $6 copying), § 324.221 for FR suspension (it is § 324.0221; § 324.221 is "Penalties"), § 322.27(5) for point thresholds (they are § 322.27(3)(a)-(c); (5) is habitual-offender revocation). Amount right plus citation wrong is still wrong.
3. **Is the number from the clerk's printed figure or the statute's components — or did I compute a total and call it the amount due?** No total without the clerk's figure or a labeled component-by-component build (see section 3).
4. **Is the agency page current?** Check its date line, cross-check the statute, and check for a legacy FAQ saying the opposite on the same site (see section 2). If the page is stale, flag it — do not quote it.
5. **Is the law current, or did it change?** Negligence limitations were 4 years before March 24, 2023; since the 2023 tort reform (ch. 2023-15) they are 2 years under § 95.11(4)(a). A correct statement of the old law is a wrong statement of the current law. Check the amendment date.
6. **Am I relaying a worker's figure as fact?** A worker's report is a claim, not evidence. Quote the statute line or the clerk's figure, or say "not verified." Never pass a worker's polished number through as checked fact.
7. **Am I stating a platform term as a legal duty?** OTR's one-business-day window, a "pending review" dashboard label, a portal's "My Cases" list — these are platform mechanics, not law. Name the source of every deadline and duty.
8. **Does an empty result prove what I think it proves?** "My Cases" showing "No items found" proves only that the logged-in account personally filed nothing through that account — an attorney's filings live in the attorney's own Bar-validated account, and the portal FAQ says so verbatim. An empty search is evidence about the search, not about the world. State what the empty result actually proves.
9. **Is this the right statute for these facts?** § 316.238 (driver conduct, failure to dim) is not § 316.237 (equipment defect) and not § 316.610 (defective equipment). A 238 ticket cannot be reduced to 237 or 610 on the same facts — 238 is a driver's choice with working lights; 237/610 require a defect. Check the conduct the section actually regulates before applying it.
10. **Can Git-FED re-check this from what I wrote?** Every asserted fact carries its section number and its source (statute, clerk figure, or agency page with date). If a claim has no citable source in the sentence, it does not go out. If it cannot be verified, it goes out labeled "not verified" — never as fact.
