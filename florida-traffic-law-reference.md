# PART 1 — FLORIDA TRAFFIC LAW

## 1. Fla. Stat. § 316.238 — Failure to dim headlights (Git-FED's current ticket)

- **What it regulates:** Driver conduct, not equipment. Within 500 feet of an oncoming vehicle, use a beam that does not project glaring rays into the oncoming driver's eyes (low beam is deemed glare-free). Within 300 feet behind another vehicle, do not use the uppermost (high) beam. — § 316.238(1)(a)-(b)
- **Moving violation:** "A violation of this section is a noncriminal traffic infraction, punishable as a moving violation as provided in chapter 318." — § 316.238(2)
- **Points: 3** on conviction (4 if it results in a crash). — § 322.27(3)(d)8; FLHSMV Appendix C (rev. 9-2-2025) lists "316.238 HEADLIGHTS – FAIL TO DIM — M, 3 points, $60 base"
- **Base fine: $60.** — § 318.18(3)(a) ("$60 for all moving violations not requiring a mandatory appearance")
- **Total payable:** $60 base plus court costs and surcharges under § 318.18; the exact total is whatever the clerk printed on the paper ticket. No statute states a single total for this offense.
- **Git-FED's ticket:** issued Sat Oct 24, 2026, 06:05 AM, Broward County, Fort Lauderdale court, 2020 Buick, citation AOEYHKE. Ticket says "must comply within 30 days."

## 2. Fla. Stat. § 316.237 — Equipment (multiple-beam road-lighting equipment)

- **What it regulates:** The equipment itself — beam design, aim, intensity, beam indicator, no coverings over headlamps. A defect exists regardless of how the driver uses the lights.
- **Non-moving violation:** "A violation of this section is a noncriminal traffic infraction, punishable as a nonmoving violation as provided in chapter 318." — § 316.237(3)
- **Points: 0. Base fine: $30.** — § 318.18(2); FLHSMV Appendix C: "316.237 HEADLIGHTS – IMPROPER — N, 0 points, $30 base"
- **Why it matters:** OTR's case-page label for Git-FED's ticket ("non-moving, 0 points, up to $129") matches § 316.237's profile exactly, not § 316.238's. The label was misattributed — the two sections are different conduct (equipment defect vs. failure to dim).
- **Could a 316.238 ticket be reduced to 316.237?** Not on the same facts. 238 is a driver's choice with working lights; 237 requires a defect in the equipment. No statute provides a mechanism to amend one charge to the other; any such change is a charging/court decision, not something the statute grants.

## 3. Fla. Stat. § 318.14 — Traffic school election (BDI)

- **How to elect:** Within 30 days of the citation date, inform the clerk of court in the county of citation *before* attending the course, and pay the election fee. In Broward: election must be submitted in writing with full payment plus a $7.00 driving-school affidavit fee. — § 318.14(9); browardclerk.org
- **18% reduction:** "Any civil penalty that is imposed by s. 318.18(3) must be reduced by 18 percent." — § 318.14(9). The reduction applies to the civil penalty under § 318.18(3), not necessarily every component of the total due.
- **Adjudication withheld, no points:** On a proper election, "adjudication must be withheld" and "points, as provided by s. 322.27, may not be assessed." — § 318.14(9). Withheld adjudication "is not a conviction." — § 318.14(11)
- **Once in 12 months:** "A person may not make an election under this subsection if the person has made an election under this subsection in the preceding 12 months." — § 318.14(9)
- **Lifetime limit: 8.** "A person may not make more than eight elections within his or her lifetime under this subsection." — § 318.14(9) (2024 Statutes, flhouse.gov, verified verbatim). The limit was 5 until it was raised to 8 effective July 1, 2024. FLHSMV's BDI page and Broward's affidavit form still say 5 and are stale on this point. One sweep worker reported the amendment backwards (claiming 5); the statute text settles it at 8.
- **90-day completion window:** Complete an approved Basic Driver Improvement course within 90 days from the election date and notify the clerk before the 90 days are up. Broward's rule (90 days from election) governs Broward cases; an older FLHSMV FAQ phrases it as "between 60 and 90 days from the date of the citation."
- **No withdrawal:** No withdrawal right appears in § 318.14(9). FLHSMV FAQ: "Once the affidavit is signed and recorded by the court, drivers may not choose the school option again for 12 months and all payments are forfeited." Broward allows forfeiting the election (appear, pay forfeit/delinquent fees, receive points) — but the election still counts and the money is lost.
- **If the course is not completed:** Driver is deemed to have admitted the infraction and is adjudicated guilty; must pay back the amount deducted by the election plus a processing fee of up to $18; points are added. — § 318.14(9); browardclerk.org
- **Paying the fine first bars election:** Paying fines/fees in full alone "is considered a conviction and points may be assessed... You will not be allowed to elect the driving school option at a later date." — browardclerk.org
- **Eligibility exclusions:** No CDL or commercial learner's permit; noncommercial vehicle; not cited for 30+ mph over the limit (§§ 316.183(2), 316.187, 316.189); not cited for §§ 320.0605, 320.07(3)(a)-(b), 322.065, 322.15(1), 322.61, 322.62.
- **Separate limit:** § 318.14(10) nolo contendere / proof-of-compliance elections have their own 3-lifetime limit — do not confuse with the § 318.14(9) school limit of 8.

## 4. Fla. Stat. § 318.15 — Failure to comply; D6 suspension

- **Trigger:** Failure to comply with the civil penalties in § 318.18 within the time in § 318.14(4); failure to enter into or comply with a penalty payment plan under §§ 318.14 and 28.246; failure to attend driver improvement school; or failure to appear at a scheduled hearing. — § 318.15(1)(a)
- **Clerk notice — 10 days:** The clerk "must notify the Department of Highway Safety and Motor Vehicles of such failure within 10 days after such failure." — § 318.15(1)(a)
- **Suspension effective — 20 days after the order:** "Upon receipt of such notice, the department must immediately issue an order suspending the driver license and privilege to drive of such person effective 20 days after the date the order of suspension is provided in accordance with s. 322.251(1), (2), and (6)." — § 318.15(1)(a)
- **Bottom line:** No suspension is effective at midnight on day 31. The 30-day deadline is only the failure date; then up to 10 days for clerk notice, then the order, then 20 more days — roughly 30 days out from the deadline.
- **Duration:** FLHSMV: license "will be suspended indefinitely" for failure to pay or comply until court requirements are met. An unreinstated suspension stays on FLHSMV records for 7 years after the date imposed, then is removed. — § 318.15(1)(a); flhsmv.gov
- **Hearing right — 180 days:** "A person who is charged with a traffic infraction may request a hearing within 180 days after the date upon which the violation occurred, regardless of any action taken by the court or the department to suspend the person's driving privilege, and, upon request, the clerk must set the case for hearing." If the 180th day is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the request must be made within 177 days; the court may grant a later request. — § 318.15(1)(c)
- **Reinstatement fee — $60:** No reinstatement until the person complies with a payment plan or with all obligations/penalties under § 318.18 and presents a court certificate of compliance "together with a nonrefundable service charge of $60 imposed under s. 322.29." — § 318.15(2); FLHSMV fee schedule lists "D-6 Suspension - DHSMV $60.00"
- **School-failure track:** A person who elected school, paid, then fails to attend is deemed to have admitted the infraction and is adjudicated guilty, must repay any 18% reduction plus a processing fee of up to $18, and points are assessed under § 322.27. — § 318.15(1)(b)

## 5. Fla. Stat. § 318.18 — Fines and fees

- **$60 base fine — moving violations:** "Except as otherwise provided in this section, $60 for all moving violations not requiring a mandatory appearance." — § 318.18(3)(a)
- **$30 base fine — non-moving violations.** — § 318.18(2)
- **$35 court costs — moving infractions:** Current § 318.18(12)(a)3 (older versions: (11)(a)3): "For moving traffic infractions: $35, from which the clerk shall remit $5 to the Department of Revenue." So $60 + $35 = $95 before other add-ons.
- **Other add-ons in the same section:** § 318.18(12)(c) adds $2.50; § 318.18(12)(d) adds $3 plus $2 "when assessed by a municipality or county." § 318.18(14)(a) allows a county surcharge of up to $30 for court facilities. Totals vary by county.
- **$16 late fee:** A person who "fails to comply with the court's requirements or who fails to pay the civil penalties specified in this section within the 30-day period provided for in s. 318.14 must pay an additional civil penalty of $16." — § 318.18(8)(a)
- **$7 driving-school affidavit fee — amount confirmed, citation corrected:** Broward Clerk: electing driving school "must be submitted in writing along with full payment and an additional $7.00 driving school affidavit fee." The $7.00 charge is § 28.24(9)(a)-(b) ("for writing any paper… including signing and sealing: 7.00"), NOT § 28.24(8) — § 28.24(8) is $6.00 for copying per page.
- **$129 and $166 — neither is a statutory total:** Neither number appears as a stated total in § 318.18(3), (8), or (12). The statute sets components, not a single total for failure to dim. $129 ($95 + $34 in add-ons) and $166 ($95 + $71) are both arithmetically reachable only after county/clerk add-ons, so either could be a clerk's printed total — but neither is verified as a statutory total. The controlling amount is on the citation / clerk notice.
- **Speeding fines (graduated):** § 318.18(3)(b): 1–5 mph over $25; 6–9 $50; 10–14 $100; 15–19 $150; 20–29 $175; 30+ $250. Second/subsequent 30+ within 12 months doubles to $500. School/construction-zone speeding doubles the amount.

## 6. Fla. Stat. § 322.27 — Point system

- **Point values — § 322.27(3)(d):** Reckless driving 4; leaving scene of crash, property damage over $50, 6; unlawful speed or unlawful wireless-device use resulting in a crash, 6; passing stopped school bus 4 (6 if serious bodily injury/death); unlawful speed 3 (≤15 mph over) / 4 (>15 mph over); traffic control signal device violation 4; driving through railroad-highway grade crossing 6; **all other moving violations 3** (including § 316.238 failure to dim); any moving violation in (d), except speed/wireless-device, resulting in a crash, 4; littering conviction § 403.413(6)(b), 3; § 316.0775(2), 4; moving violation + unlawful wireless-device use in a school safety zone, +2.
- **Suspension thresholds — § 322.27(3)(a)-(c):** 12 points within 12 months = suspension of not more than 30 days; 18 points within 18 months (including points from the (a) suspension) = not more than 3 months; 24 points within 36 months (including points from (a) and (b)) = not more than 1 year. No point suspension may exceed 1 year.
- **How points are counted:** Points and time periods are computed using the **offense date** of each conviction, not the conviction date. — § 322.27(4)
- **Out-of-state violations count:** A conviction in another state (or under substantially conforming federal law) for an offense that would violate Florida traffic law is recorded at the same number of points as if convicted in Florida. — § 322.27(3)(e)
- **Point reduction after suspension:** 3 points are deducted from the record of a person whose privilege was suspended only once under § 322.27(3) and reinstated, if all other requirements were met. — § 322.27(3)(h)
- **How long points stay:** No single fixed period in § 322.27. Points count toward suspension only inside the 12/18/36-month windows. FLHSMV: "Citation dispositions of guilty — Most non-moving/moving violations have a 3- to 5-year retention; for more serious violations the retention periods vary (10 years, 15 years, etc.)."
- **Point suspension vs. court-ordered suspension:** A point suspension is an administrative FLHSMV action triggered automatically by point totals. A court-ordered suspension is different: under § 322.27(2), FLHSMV **shall suspend upon direction of the court** after a conviction, when the court finds the seriousness of the offense and circumstances warrant it — no point threshold required.
- **Correction:** The point system is § 322.27(3), not (5). Subsection (5) covers habitual-offender revocation (minimum 5 years to relicense).
- **Reinstatement after point suspension:** FLHSMV requires completion of an Advanced Driver Improvement (ADI) course if the license was suspended for accumulation of points, as a non-DUI habitual traffic offender, or by court order.

## 7. Fla. Stat. § 316.610 — Defective equipment

- **What it covers:** Driving, or knowingly permitting to be driven, a vehicle in unsafe condition, or without the lamps/equipment required by Ch. 316 in proper condition and adjustment, or equipped in violation of Ch. 316.
- **Non-moving, $30 base:** § 318.18(2) is headed "Thirty dollars for all nonmoving traffic violations," and (2)(c) expressly lists "all violations of ss. 316.2935 and 316.610" in that group. — § 318.18(2)(c)
- **Enforcement:** An officer may stop and inspect on reasonable cause. If continued operation would be unduly hazardous, the vehicle may be required to be immediately repaired/removed; if not hazardous (e.g. tailpipes, mufflers, windshield wipers, marginally worn tires), the officer gives written notice to repair within 48 hours, excluding Sunday. — § 316.610(1)-(2)
- **"Fix-it" reduction — §§ 316.6105, 318.18(2)(c):** If the defect is corrected and proof is obtained by an affidavit of compliance from law enforcement within 30 days of citation, plus $4 paid to the agency, the fine "must be reduced to $10," retained by the clerk. Procedure: officer issues the affidavit form with the citation; driver repairs and presents the vehicle to local police/sheriff for inspection within 30 days. — § 316.6105(1)-(3)
- **Is 316.238 → 316.610 the "standard" plea reduction? NOT VERIFIED in primary sources.** No Online Sunshine, FLHSMV, or Broward Clerk source states that this specific swap is standard, routine, or a statutory entitlement. It would be a negotiated/court disposition, not a right in §§ 316.610, 316.238, or 318.18. If the conviction is amended to § 316.610 (non-moving), it falls outside the § 322.27(3)(d) point schedule, so 0 points instead of 3.
- **Insurance note:** FLHSMV states insurers cannot impose an additional premium or refuse to renew solely because of a non-criminal traffic infraction when adjudication has been withheld and no points have been assessed, with stated exceptions (e.g. a crash where the insurer was at fault and incurred a loss).

## 8. Fla. Rule of General Practice & Judicial Administration 2.514 — Day counting

- **Current rule (renamed from Fla. R. Jud. Admin. 2.514):** For a period stated in days, "(A) begin counting from the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday; (B) count every day, including intermediate Saturdays, Sundays, and legal holidays." — Rule 2.514(a)(1)(A)-(B) (Jan. 1, 2026 edition)
- **Weekend/holiday last day:** "If the last day is a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday, the period continues to run until the end of the next day that is not a Saturday, Sunday, or legal holiday." — Rule 2.514(a)(1)(C)
- **Application to Git-FED's ticket (issued Sat Oct 24, 2026):**
  - Under the current rule: trigger Sat Oct 24 → next non-weekend day is Mon Oct 26 = day 1 → **day 30 = Tue Nov 24, 2026**.
  - Under simple calendar counting (exclude Oct 24, count every day): day 1 is Sun Oct 25 → **day 30 = Mon Nov 23, 2026**.
  - **Nov 22 is wrong under every version.** It counts the trigger day itself as day 1 and lands on a Sunday, which the rule rolls forward. Muse3's "window closed Nov 22" was the include-the-trigger-day error.
- **Holidays in the window do not move the date:** Veterans' Day, Wed Nov 11, 2026, is a § 110.117 holiday but intermediate, so it counts under (a)(1)(B). Thanksgiving (Thu Nov 26) and the Friday after (Nov 27) fall after the deadline.
- **Scope caveat:** Rule 2.514(a) applies only to a period in a rule, local rule, court order, or statute "that does not specify a method of computing time." If the citation's governing traffic statute or traffic-court rule specifies its own counting, that controls. Broward Clerk says civil traffic infractions must be paid / an option elected within **30 calendar days** from the citation date. **Safe course: treat Mon Nov 23 as the deadline.**
- **Precedent:** Judgment entered Fri June 2, 2023; counting began Mon June 5, 2023; day 30 was July 4 (legal holiday), so the deadline extended to Wed July 5, 2023, and the filing was timely. — Carpenter v. BVK-Muirfield Village, Nos. 2D2024-0589, 2D2024-1554 (Fla. 2d DCA July 11, 2025)
- **Note:** The +5 days in Rule 2.514(b) applies only after service by mail/email, not to a citation issued in person.

## 9. Broward County Clerk — Traffic procedures

- **Traffic page:** https://www.browardclerk.org/Divisions/TrafficAndMisdemeanor
- **Affidavit PDF, form CLK/TR.002 Rev. 05/12/2026:** https://www.browardclerk.org/PDF/TrafficAndMisdemeanor/Driving_School_Affidavit.pdf
- **Deadline to choose:** Within 30 calendar days of receiving the citation, choose pay in full, elect driving school, request a hearing, or conditional plea.
- **How to elect school:** Election must be submitted **in writing, with full payment plus an additional $7.00 driving-school affidavit fee**. "You may pay and elect Driving School online or in person." The Clerk's online Fine Payment service lets a defendant pay the fine **and, if they choose, elect driving school in the same transaction**. Fine Payment link resolves to https://www.browardclerk.org/Web2. Also listed: PayIt at payitbrowardclerk.payitgov.com, AMSCOT, mail, in person.
- **Affidavit filing window:** A completed affidavit may be submitted at any courthouse **within 30 days from the date the citation was paid**, or mailed to Clerk of Court, P.O. Box 14610, Fort Lauderdale, FL 33302-4610. (This applies to filing the affidavit after a pay-and-elect transaction, not to converting a standalone pay-in-full into a school election later.)
- **$7 fee authority:** The affidavit states, per F.S. 28.24(8), a $7.00 fee is charged when selecting the driving-school option — but see § 5 above: the $7 charge is actually § 28.24(9)(a)-(b); § 28.24(8) is $6.00 for copying. The affidavit form's citation is wrong; the amount is right.
- **How to pay a fine online:** Online payment is only for the **entire amount due**; partial payment online is only for cases with established payment plans. Processing/transaction fees may apply.
- **90-day completion window:** Complete a Florida-approved Basic Driver Improvement course **within 90 days from the election date**, and notify/file proof with the Clerk **before the 90 days are up**. Out-of-state certificates are not accepted.
- **Proof is the driver's responsibility:** File the certificate via the Florida Courts E-Filing Portal, in person, or by mail (original). Email and fax copies are not accepted. Even if a school sends it electronically, the defendant must ensure the Clerk receives it within 90 days.
- **Eligibility (4-hour course):** No election in the past 12 months, no more than 5 elections in a lifetime (form's number — stale; statute says 8), no CDL, and not charged with exceeding the speed limit by more than 30 mph. An 8-hour option exists for those no longer eligible for the 4-hour course.
- **If you pay the fine first, then try to elect school:** Paying fines/fees in full alone "is considered a conviction and points may be assessed... **You will not be allowed to elect the driving school option at a later date.**"
- **If you fail to finish in 90 days:** Points, additional fees, and possible license suspension; the defendant must pay the difference in the adjusted fine plus an $18 processing fee. An extension may be requested in person within 90 days of election (extra fee if requested after 90 days).

## 10. Florida Courts E-Filing Portal — "My Cases"

- **Portal URL:** https://www.myflcourtaccess.com/
- **"My Cases" shows only cases YOU filed in, with THAT account.** The portal FAQ states verbatim: "My Cases — Contains all the cases to which you have filed on the portal with this portal account." It is not a list of all cases you are a party to.
- **"My Submissions"** is the companion list: "Contains all of the filings you have submitted to the portal with this portal account," including tracking through the clerk's docketing process and any filings returned for correction.
- **An attorney's filings land in the attorney's account, not the client's.** Attorney portal accounts are validated against The Florida Bar member database (name + Bar number); even a paralegal filing for an attorney files under an account carrying that attorney's name and Bar number. A filing the lawyer submits appears in the lawyer's "My Cases"/"My Submissions," not in the client's.
- **You cannot check someone else's filings from your own portal account.** The portal FAQ: "Can we do a search for cases that are not ours? No. Remote electronic access to the official court file is allowed only to counsel of record on a case."
- **The way to verify a filing is the county clerk's case system, not the portal.** The portal links to the county's case maintenance system (CMS) from the My Cases page (click the case number) or My Filings page (click Case Style/Docket). Without a case in My Cases, that link is unavailable, so the check must be done directly through the county clerk's public records search using the case number or party name.
- **E-service is separate from filing.** Someone can be added to a case's e-service list by another filer and receive filings by email (Notices of Electronic Filing) without ever filing. Not being on the service list proves nothing either.
- **CaseAccess** is a paid statewide search subscription through the portal ($20/month or $200/year) that searches clerk-sourced case information from all 67 counties, updated daily from CCIS. It is a research tool, not the official court record — the clerk remains the source of the official record.
- **FLAG: "No items found" does NOT prove his lawyer filed nothing.** Three independent reasons: (1) "My Cases" only lists cases the logged-in account itself filed to — his lawyer's filings sit in the lawyer's own Bar-validated account, so his page would show "No items found" even if the lawyer filed ten documents; (2) merely being a party to a case never adds it to "My Cases" — only filing to it with that account does; (3) it also misses paper/in-person filings and anything filed before the account existed. To actually verify: ask the lawyer for the filing confirmation emails/Notices of Electronic Filing, or search the county clerk's public case records by name/case number (or use a CaseAccess subscription). An empty "My Cases" proves only that *he personally* has filed nothing through *that* account.

## 11. Off the Record (OTR) — Platform rules

- **Sources:** OTR's own site (offtherecord.com KB / terms), read Nov 23, 2026.
- **"Pending review":** SmartMatch has matched an attorney, who is notified and reviews the case; the matched attorney is shown in the dashboard immediately and the case chat opens at once.
- **Acceptance window — one full business day:** The attorney has **one full business day** to accept or decline; if they fail to do so within one business day OTR may reassign the case. OTR's own example: Friday 1 PM booking → window open until Monday 1 PM. Booked Sat Nov 21, 2026, the first business day is Mon Nov 23.
- **No attorney-client relationship before acceptance:** "The attorney-client relationship is not formed until the attorney accepts your case." Until then the attorney is only reviewing fit. "Until they accept, you do not yet have official legal representation."
- **Booking is a hold, not a charge:** Booking only places a **pre-authorization hold** (legal fee + service fee, up to 7 days); it is captured as a charge only once an attorney accepts. If cancelled/no attorney before acceptance, the hold is released and you are never charged.
- **Decline / rematch:** On decline, SmartMatch **automatically** selects the next best available attorney; this repeats until a firm accepts or no appropriate attorneys remain. No action needed from the client. Per round: the new attorney gets one full business day to review, and rematching itself "can take up to one business day" while SmartMatch identifies the next attorney.
- **If no attorney accepts:** OTR cancels the case and issues a **full refund of any fees paid**.
- **Guarantee — legal fee only:** The money-back guarantee refunds the **legal fee only** ($98 here) when an eligible case is lost with no improvement in charge, points, or fine. It "does not cover: court fines, fees, or costs" or payments outside OTR. The **$10 OTR service fee is therefore not covered by the guarantee** (no OTR page says the service fee is refunded on a loss). Contrast: cancelling within the 1-business-day window refunds **everything, including the service fee**.
- **What voids the guarantee (5 situations):** Paying the ticket or resolving the case yourself (paying = pleading guilty, ends the case; "settling the ticket on your own voids your refund"); not responding in time if the loss results from that; missing a required court date; not completing required post-resolution steps; refusing a favorable outcome your attorney negotiated.
- **Traffic school and the guarantee:** Merely registering for an online driving course is **not listed** among the 5 voiding actions. Electing school yourself with the court is different: that resolves the case yourself, which voids the guarantee. Completing a course as a step your attorney requires *after* resolution is required, not voiding. **Traffic school secured BY the attorney counts as a win** — listed as a winning outcome; no refund applies. OTR's example: attorney making a previously-ineligible ticket eligible for traffic school/deferral is a success.
- **Florida cases** are handled by OTR Florida Law, with the same guarantee.

## 12. Driving school course rules — Registration vs. election

- **Registering for a course does NOT elect traffic school and does NOT notify the court.** Election is a separate act done with the clerk: drivers must "voluntarily elect this option within 30 days [of the] citation date and, prior to attending the course, drivers must inform the clerk of court in the county where the citation was issued of this election," plus pay an election fee. Contacting a course provider to register is a later, separate step. — flhsmv.gov BDI page
- **Election deadline is 30 days from the citation date.** "Drivers may not elect to take the BDI, in lieu of points, after the initial 30-day period." If no election is made, the right is forfeited and points are assessed.
- **Notifying the clerk of completion is the DRIVER's responsibility, not the school's.** Broward: "You MUST notify the Broward Clerk of Courts of your completed school before the 90 days are up. (This is your responsibility, not the driving school)." Even though "some driving schools may electronically send the certificate," the driver must ensure the clerk receives it. FLHSMV FAQ agrees: "It is the driver's responsibility to present the completion certificate to the clerk's office."
- **Completion window in Broward: 90 days from the election date.** Statewide, FLHSMV's FAQ frames it as "between 60 and 90 days from the date of the citation" depending on the county; Broward's own rule (90 days from election) governs Broward cases.
- **Proof of course registration does NOT count as an election.** No primary source treats registration/enrollment as an election. The sequence in every source is election with the clerk first (affidavit + payment, within 30 days), then register/attend the course.
- **If the course is not completed in time:** the driver "is considered to have admitted to the infraction and is adjudicated as guilty," must pay back the amount deducted from the fee because of the election plus a processing fee of up to $18, and points are added to the record. Broward adds: points, additional fees, and a possible license suspension. Broward allows an extension request in person within 90 days of the election (extra fee if requested after 90 days).

## 13. License sanctions — FR suspension and FR cancellation

- **Correction:** The operative statute is **§ 324.0221**, not § 324.221. § 324.221 is "Penalties" in ch. 324. § 322.26 is **not** the FR insurance statute — it is mandatory *revocation* on conviction (DUI manslaughter, vehicular homicide, felony with a vehicle, etc.).
- **FR suspension is the sanction; "FR cancellation" is the trigger.** Under § 324.0221(2), FLHSMV "shall suspend" both the registration *and* driver license when (a) its records show required security was not in force, or (b) the insurer notifies it of cancellation/termination of required security. An "FR-CANCELLATION" entry means a PIP cancellation was reported and a suspension is pending; the suspension itself is the FR sanction.
- **Minimum coverage — $10,000 PIP + $10,000 PDL, continuous:** Florida's No-Fault Law requires a registered motor vehicle to carry at least $10,000 personal injury protection (PIP) and $10,000 property damage liability (PDL) throughout the registration period. Failure to maintain it may suspend the license/registration, with a $150–$500 reinstatement fee. — §§ 627.733, 324.022
- **Insurer must report cancellation to FLHSMV within 10 days:** An insurer issuing PIP/PDL must report cancellation or nonrenewal to the department within 10 days after the processing or effective date; failure is a Florida Insurance Code violation. — § 324.0221(1)(a)
- **The "10-day notice" is insurer-to-FLHSMV, not a 10-day grace period for the driver:** The insurer must also notify the named insured in writing that cancellation will be reported to FLHSMV, that failure to maintain PIP/PDL may result in loss of registration and driving privileges, and state the reinstatement-fee amounts. — § 324.0221(1)(b)
- **FLHSMV then notifies the driver:** If the insurer notifies FLHSMV of cancellation/removal, no other active Florida policy is reported, and the vehicle has a valid Florida registration, FLHSMV sends a letter requiring proof of coverage.
- **FR suspension:** After due notice and an opportunity to be heard, FLHSMV "shall suspend" the registration and driver license of the owner/registrant when its records show required security under §§ 324.022 and 627.733 was not in force, or on insurer notification of cancellation/termination. — § 324.0221(2)
- **Avoiding the fee/suspension:** If the Notice of Suspension requirements are met before the original suspension date on the notice, no reinstatement fee is required. If not met by that date, a fee is due.
- **Reinstatement fees (nonrefundable):** $150 first reinstatement; $250 second; $500 each subsequent reinstatement within 3 years after the first. If no second reinstatement within 3 years, the fee resets to $150. Only one fee reinstates both license and registration. — § 324.0221(3)
- **Reinstatement coverage — noncancelable, 2 years, not automatically SR-22:** A person reinstating under § 324.0221(3) must secure noncancelable coverage as described in §§ 324.021(8), 324.023 and 627.7275(2), present proof it is in force on a department form, and maintain that proof for 2 years. — § 324.0221(3)
- **SR-22 is a different, crash-related requirement:** FLHSMV requires an SR-22 filing (insurer-certified liability insurance) for 3 years for an at-fault driver in an injury crash without the required $10,000/$20,000/$10,000 coverage — not as the standard cure for a PIP-cancellation (FR7) case.
- **Alternative to re-insuring:** Surrender the Florida tag/registration immediately on cancelling insurance if the vehicle is no longer owned, inoperable, or not driven on public roadways.
- **How to prove coverage:** Insurers report initiation/cancellation electronically to FLHSMV. The driver can update insurance online via MyDMV Portal / Driver License Check (mydmvportal.flhsmv.gov), by phone at 850-617-2000, by mail, or in person at a driver license / tax collector office; the FR case number from the Notice of Suspension is needed to pay a fee online.

## 14. Traffic infraction court process — Plea options

- **Three practical options, within 30 days:** Pay the civil penalty; elect Basic Driver Improvement (BDI) traffic school; or elect to appear / request a hearing to contest. — § 318.14(4)-(5), (9)
- **Guilty / pay = admission:** A person who does not elect to appear must pay within 30 days after issuance; doing so is deemed an admission and waives the hearing right. — § 318.14(4). Broward: paying is considered a conviction and points may be assessed.
- **No contest (nolo contendere):** For listed compliance offenses only (e.g. no valid license/registration, no insurance proof), a driver may elect nolo contendere and provide proof of compliance to the clerk; adjudication shall be withheld. — § 318.14(10). Broward also offers a Conditional Plea of No Contest / Waiver of Appearance for eligible civil infractions not involving a crash or mandatory appearance.
- **Not guilty / contest:** Electing to appear leads to a hearing before the designated official; the infraction must be proved beyond a reasonable doubt. — § 318.14(5)-(6). Broward: request a hearing in writing to the Clerk within 30 calendar days of the citation; you will be notified of the court date.
- **Adjudication withheld — meaning:** It is not a conviction. — § 318.14(11). Available (a) mandatorily on a valid BDI election, § 318.14(9); (b) on a nolo-contendere compliance election, § 318.14(10); (c) at a hearing / conditional no-contest plea if the judge or hearing officer so orders, per Broward.
- **No-contest + withheld — points and insurance:** No points may be assessed under § 322.27 on a BDI election, § 318.14(9); FLHSMV: "Points may not be added … and adjudication of guilt is withheld." FLHSMV also states an insurer cannot impose an additional premium, cancel, or non-renew solely because of an infraction when adjudication is withheld and no points were assessed, unless it involved a crash where the insurer was at fault and incurred a loss.
- **Arraignment vs. hearing:** Civil infractions have no criminal arraignment. A criminal traffic or misdemeanor charge requires a court appearance; a civil infraction can usually be disposed of by payment or BDI election with no appearance. A hearing is the contested proceeding under § 318.14(5): if proven, penalty up to $500 ($1,000 for school/construction zone), driver-improvement school, or both; if not proven, no costs/penalties.
- **Deadline / failure to act:** 30 calendar days from citation issuance/receipt to pay, elect school, or request a hearing in writing. Failure to pay may result in license suspension plus late/reinstatement fees.
- **FLAG — "lawyer is your only way to avoid points" is incorrect.** Traffic-school election alone avoids points, without a lawyer, if eligible: FLHSMV requires voluntarily electing BDI within 30 days, informing the clerk before attending, and completing an approved course; then "adjudication must be withheld" and "points … may not be assessed." Exclusions: CDL holders, 30+ mph over limit, an election in the preceding 12 months.

## 15. Speeding 30–49 mph over the limit

- **Charged statute: § 316.183, "Unlawful speed."** § 316.183(2) sets the posted/maximum limits; § 316.183(7) makes a violation "a noncriminal traffic infraction, punishable as a moving violation as provided in chapter 318."
- **Points: 4 points (no crash).** § 322.27(3)(d)5.b: "Unlawful speed… In excess of 15 miles per hour of lawful or posted speed—4 points." 30–49 over is in that tier; 3 points is only ≤15 over. If unlawful speed results in a crash, 6 points under § 322.27(3)(d)3.
- **Base fine: $250.** § 318.18(3)(b) fine schedule: "30 mph and above..........$250." A second/subsequent conviction for 30+ within 12 months doubles it to $500 under § 318.18(3); school/construction-zone speeding doubles the § 318.18(3)(b) amount.
- **Typical total: $250 base + court costs/surcharges, county-specific — no single statewide total in the primary sources checked.** FLHSMV Appendix C lists the same $250 base for "30 & above MPH over limit." Do not treat $250 as the amount due.
- **Mandatory court appearance: Yes.** § 318.19(5): "Any infraction of s. 316.183(2), s. 316.187, or s. 316.189 of exceeding the speed limit by 30 mph or more" requires a mandatory hearing; the person "shall not have the provisions of s. 318.14(2), (4), and (9) available… but must appear." FLHSMV Appendix C: "Effective Oct. 1, 2006, Mandatory Hearing when speed exceeds limit by 30 mph or more."
- **Traffic school (BDI) election: No.** § 318.14(9) allows BDI election only for infractions "other than a violation of s. 316.183(2), s. 316.187, or s. 316.189 when the driver exceeds the posted limit by 30 miles per hour or more." FLHSMV and Broward Clerk agree: BDI is unavailable if cited at 30 mph over (Broward/FLHSMV wording says "more than 30 mph"; the statute controls at 30 or more).

## 16. Driving while license suspended — § 322.34

- **Statute:** § 322.34, "Driving while license suspended, revoked, canceled, or disqualified," covers suspended, revoked, and canceled licenses in one section.
- **Criminal vs. civil infraction — the knowledge element is the dividing line:**
  - Unknowing: driving while suspended/revoked/canceled without knowledge is a noncriminal moving violation, punishable under ch. 318 (civil traffic fine, no jail, no criminal record). — § 322.34(1)
  - Knowing: driving while knowing of the cancellation/suspension/revocation is a crime. — § 322.34(2)
  - Knowledge is satisfied if the driver was previously cited under subsection (1), admitted knowledge, or received notice under subsection (4); there is also a rebuttable presumption of knowledge if a suspension order appears in DHSMV records, except for failure-to-pay-fine or financial-responsibility suspensions. — § 322.34(2), (4)
- **Penalties — knowing DWLS:**
  - 1st conviction: 2nd-degree misdemeanor, up to 60 days jail and up to $500 fine. — § 322.34(2)(a); § 775.082(4)(b); § 775.083(1)(e)
  - 2nd or subsequent conviction: 1st-degree misdemeanor, up to 1 year jail and up to $1,000 fine. — § 322.34(2)(b)1.; § 775.082(4)(a); § 775.083(1)(d)
  - 3rd or subsequent conviction: minimum 10 days jail. — § 322.34(2)(b)2.
  - 3rd or subsequent conviction is a 3rd-degree felony (up to 5 years, up to $5,000) ONLY if the current or most recent prior violation stems from DUI, refusal to submit to a urine/breath/blood test, a traffic offense causing death or serious bodily injury, or fleeing/eluding. — § 322.34(2)(c)
  - Driving while designated a habitual traffic offender: 3rd-degree felony on any conviction. — § 322.34(5)
  - DWLS plus careless/negligent operation causing death or serious bodily injury: 3rd-degree felony. — § 322.34(6)
  - Commercial motor vehicle: 1st conviction 1st-degree misdemeanor; 2nd+ 3rd-degree felony. — § 322.34(7)
- **Financial-obligation suspensions are capped at misdemeanor:** If the suspension was for child support, other financial obligations, failure to comply with a civil penalty, failure to maintain financial responsibility, or a minor's attendance requirements (and the driver has no prior forcible felony), a knowing 1st conviction is a 2nd-degree misdemeanor and a 2nd+ is a 1st-degree misdemeanor — no felony escalation. — § 322.34(10)
- **Dismissal / withhold routes:**
  - Non-CDL driver cited for knowing DWLS on a § 322.34(10)(a) ground may plead nolo contendere and give the clerk proof of compliance (e.g., reinstated license); adjudication is withheld and that withhold "is not a conviction." Limit: no election if one was made in the preceding 12 months; max 3 elections total. — § 322.34(11)
  - Parallel clerk procedure for the unknowing charge: a driver cited under § 322.34(1) for a license suspended for failure to appear, failure to pay a civil penalty, or failure to attend driver improvement may plead nolo and present proof of compliance (a valid, renewed, or reinstated license) before the court date; adjudication withheld, not a conviction. — § 318.14(10)
  - If the license was in fact valid at the time of the stop, no violation occurred — the state's case fails on the element that the privilege was suspended/revoked/canceled.
- **Suspended vs. revoked:** Suspension is a temporary withdrawal (e.g., up to 1 year for a non-DUI violation causing death/serious bodily injury); reinstatement is by paying a reinstatement fee once the period expires. Revocation terminates the privilege — e.g., a habitual traffic offender's license is revoked for 5 years, with a hardship license possible only after 1 year. For the DWLS charge itself, § 322.34 treats suspended, revoked, and canceled identically; the difference matters in the underlying reinstatement path and in the HTO felony under subsection (5).
- **Fit to Git-FED's Nov 2024 Palm Beach Gardens ticket:** A dismissal is consistent with the § 322.34(11) or § 318.14(10) route — reinstated license plus nolo plea and proof of compliance to the clerk, with adjudication withheld (not a conviction) — or with the state being unable to prove knowledge. The statute does not state which applied in his case.

## 17. Point suspension thresholds — § 322.27(3)

- **12 points in 12 months = suspension of not more than 30 days** — § 322.27(3)(a)
- **18 points in 18 months = suspension of not more than 3 months**, including points already used for the 12-point suspension — § 322.27(3)(b)
- **24 points in 36 months = suspension of not more than 1 year**, including points already used under (a) and (b) — § 322.27(3)(c)
- **Trigger and ceiling:** FLHSMV is authorized to suspend once a driver is convicted of violations amounting to 12+ points; no point suspension may exceed 1 year — § 322.27(3) (intro)
- **How points are counted:** Points and time periods are computed using the **offense date** of each conviction, not the conviction date — § 322.27(4)
- **Out-of-state violations count:** A conviction in another state (or under substantially conforming federal law) for an offense that would violate Florida traffic law is recorded at the same number of points as if convicted in Florida, except a violation of § 322.26 — § 322.27(3)(e). Separately, committing an out-of-state offense that would be grounds for suspension/revocation in Florida is itself a basis for FLHSMV action — § 322.27(1)(e)
- **How points are avoided (traffic school):** Points are *prevented*, not erased after the fact. A driver may voluntarily elect Basic Driver Improvement (BDI) **within 30 days of the citation date**, notify the clerk of court in the citation county, and complete an FLHSMV-approved course; adjudication is then withheld and **no points are assessed** — § 318.14(4), (9)
- **How points are reduced after a suspension:** **3 points are deducted** from the record of a person whose privilege was suspended **only once** under § 322.27(3) and reinstated, if all other requirements were met — § 322.27(3)(h). There is no general "points expire after X years" rule in § 322.27; points fall out of a threshold only when they are outside the 12/18/36-month window measured from offense date, while the convictions themselves remain on the record subject to FLHSMV retention periods.

## 19. State-approved traffic schools and online course rules

- **State approval (FLHSMV):** FLHSMV "shall approve and regulate the courses of all driver improvement schools," including courses delivered by technology; approval weighs safety/crash-avoidance content, review of all course materials, provider presentation, and oversight plan. New courses are provisionally approved for one pilot judicial circuit until fully approved for statewide delivery; online courses are considered only if approved for statewide delivery. — § 318.1451(1)-(2)
- **Approved lists:** FLHSMV publishes the approved BDI and ADI provider lists; the department "shall not accept proof of attendance" from a school that does not teach an approved course. — § 318.1451(3); lists at flhsmv.gov (BDI/ADI provider pages)

- **Online BDI course:** A 4-hour course. FLHSMV calls it a "state-approved 4-hour basic driver improvement course."
- **Gap — not verified from primary sources:** The identity-verification method and final-exam pass score are not stated on the FLHSMV pages read or in §§ 318.14, 322.0261, 318.1451; they are set by department rule. Providers must submit completion info through the Driver Improvement Certificate Issuance System within 5 days. — § 318.1451(6)(f)

- **BDI vs. ADI:**
  - BDI: 4-hour course; voluntary election to avoid points, or mandatory for specified offenses under § 322.0261 (crash with hospital transport, second crash in 2 years over $500 property damage, red light, school bus, racing, reckless driving); 90 days to complete or the license is canceled.
  - ADI: 12-hour course, required when the license was suspended for points (12 pts/12 mo = 30 days; 18/18 = 3 months; 24/36 = 1 year), as a habitual traffic offender (non-DUI), or by court order. Three crashes in 3 years requires 12-hour ADI plus 4 hours behind-the-wheel plus an extended road test.

## 20. Other Florida traffic statutes (driver with multiple tickets)

- **Reckless driving — § 316.192, criminal, moving, 4 points.** Willful/wanton disregard for safety; fleeing a law-enforcement officer is reckless driving per se. 1st conviction: fine $25–$500 and/or up to 90 days jail; 2nd/subsequent: $50–$1,000 and/or up to 6 months; +$5 EMS surcharge. Causing property/person damage = 1st-degree misdemeanor; serious bodily injury = 3rd-degree felony. Points: 4, per § 322.27(3)(d)1.
- **DUI — § 316.193, criminal.** Guilty if driving/in actual physical control while normal faculties impaired, or BAC 0.08+ g/100 mL blood, or breath-alcohol 0.08+ g/210 L breath. **No points value in the § 322.27 schedule; sanction is license revocation** under § 322.28(2)(a): 1st 180 days–1 year, 2nd within 5 years min. 5 years, 3rd within 10 years min. 10 years. Fines/jail (standard): 1st $500–$1,000, up to 6 months; 2nd $1,000–$2,000, up to 9 months + 1-year ignition interlock; 3rd within 10 years = 3rd-degree felony + 2-year interlock; 3rd >10 years $2,000–$5,000, up to 12 months; 4th+ = 3rd-degree felony, fine ≥$2,000. Enhanced (BAL 0.15+ or passenger under 18): 1st $1,000–$2,000, up to 9 months; 2nd $2,000–$4,000, up to 12 months; 3rd+ ≥$4,000. With damage = 1st-degree misdemeanor; serious bodily injury = 3rd-degree felony; DUI manslaughter = 2nd-degree felony (1st-degree if driver left the scene or has a prior), 4-year mandatory minimum. Also: probation, DUI program, 50 hours community service (1st), vehicle impoundment 10/30/90 days.
- **Red light / traffic control signal — § 316.075, noncriminal infraction, moving, 4 points.** Steady red: stop before the crosswalk/intersection and remain stopped until green (right turn on red permitted after stop, yielding, unless prohibited). § 316.075(4): violation is a noncriminal traffic infraction, a moving violation if from operating a vehicle. Points: 4 for violating § 316.075(1)(c)1, per § 322.27(3)(d)6. Fine: $60 base per § 318.18(3)(a), plus court costs/fees. FLHSMV notes a conviction requires a driver-improvement course.
- **Failure to yield — §§ 316.121, 316.123, 316.125, noncriminal infraction, moving, 3 points.** § 316.121: yield to a vehicle already in the intersection / on the right when entering simultaneously. § 316.123: stop at a stop intersection, then yield; at a yield sign, slow/stop as needed, then yield. § 316.125(3) (entering from private road/driveway/alley): expressly "a noncriminal traffic infraction, punishable as a moving violation as provided in chapter 318." Points: 3 as "all other moving violations," § 322.27(3)(d)7; fine $60 base per § 318.18(3)(a).
- **Careless driving — § 316.1925, noncriminal infraction, moving, 3 points.** Must drive "in a careful and prudent manner" for width, grade, curves, corners, traffic, and all attendant circumstances. § 316.1925(2): "cited for a moving violation, punishable as provided in chapter 318." Fine $60 base per § 318.18(3)(a); points 3 per § 322.27(3)(d)7.
- **Leaving the scene — §§ 316.061–316.064 (plus § 316.027), criminal.** § 316.061 (crash, property damage only, attended vehicle/property): must stop, remain, and fulfill § 316.062; violation = 2nd-degree misdemeanor (+$5 EMS). Points: 6 for leaving the scene of a crash with property damage over $50, § 322.27(3)(d)2. § 316.062: duty to give information and render aid. § 316.063: duty on damaging an unattended vehicle/property. § 316.064: when the driver is unable to report. § 316.027 (crash with injury/death): must stop/remain; willful violation is a felony — 2006 text: injury = 3rd-degree felony, death = 1st-degree felony, license revoked. (Re-check current § 316.027 text: later amendments split injury / serious bodily injury / death into 3rd / 2nd / 1st-degree felonies.)
- **Infraction vs. criminal traffic offense — §§ 318.13, 318.14, 318.17.** § 318.13(3): "infraction" = a noncriminal violation, not punishable by incarceration, with no right to jury trial or court-appointed counsel. § 318.14(1): most ch. 316 violations are charged as noncriminal infractions, except as provided in § 318.17. § 318.17 excepts from ch. 318: fleeing/eluding (§ 316.1935), leaving the scene (§§ 316.027, 316.061), DUI (§ 316.193), reckless driving (§ 316.192), false crash reports, and "any other offense in chapter 316 which is classified as a criminal violation." Practical line: infractions = civil penalty + points; § 318.17 offenses = criminal (jail possible, mandatory court, no traffic-school election under ch. 318).
- **Fine/points baselines — §§ 318.18, 322.27:** $30 for nonmoving violations; $60 for all moving violations not requiring a mandatory appearance (speeding is separately graduated, $25–$250). Point schedule: reckless 4, leaving scene (property over $50) 6, unlawful speed in a crash 6, traffic-control signal 4, all other moving violations 3; any moving violation (except speeding) resulting in a crash 4. # PART 3 — GENERAL FLORIDA LAW

## 21. Criminal law — Offense classifications

- **Felony vs. misdemeanor defined:** A felony is any offense punishable by death or imprisonment in a state penitentiary; a misdemeanor is punishable by a term in a county correctional facility not exceeding 1 year. — § 775.08
- **Felony degrees:** Capital felony; life felony; 1st, 2nd, and 3rd degree. A felony declared without a degree is 3rd degree (except life-punishable first offenses). **Misdemeanor degrees:** 1st and 2nd degree; an unspecified misdemeanor is 2nd degree. — § 775.081
- **Maximum imprisonment — § 775.082:** Capital felony: death, otherwise life imprisonment, ineligible for parole. Life felony (on/after July 1, 1995): life imprisonment or a term of years not exceeding life. 1st-degree felony: up to 30 years, or up to life where specifically provided. 2nd-degree felony: up to 15 years. 3rd-degree felony: up to 5 years. 1st-degree misdemeanor: up to 1 year. 2nd-degree misdemeanor: up to 60 days. Noncriminal violation: no imprisonment; punishment no more severe than a fine, forfeiture, or other civil penalty (except as provided in ch. 316 or by local ordinance).
- **Maximum fines — § 775.083(1):** Life felony $15,000; 1st- or 2nd-degree felony $10,000; 3rd-degree felony $5,000; 1st-degree misdemeanor $1,000; 2nd-degree misdemeanor or noncriminal violation $500; plus double pecuniary gain/loss where applicable.
- **Theft — § 812.014:** $100,000+ = grand theft, 1st-degree felony; $20,000–$100,000 = grand theft, 2nd-degree felony; $750–$20,000 = grand theft, 3rd-degree felony; $100–$750 = petit theft, 1st-degree misdemeanor; under $100 (no prior) = petit theft, 2nd-degree misdemeanor. A prior theft conviction raises petit theft to a 1st-degree misdemeanor; a third petit-theft conviction is a 3rd-degree felony. Certain property (e.g., firearm, motor vehicle) is grand theft regardless of value.
- **Battery — § 784.03:** Intentionally touching/striking another against their will, or intentionally causing bodily harm, is ordinarily a 1st-degree misdemeanor. A second or subsequent battery after one prior battery/aggravated battery/felony battery conviction is a 3rd-degree felony; battery in furtherance of a riot/aggravated riot is also a 3rd-degree felony. Felony battery (great bodily harm, permanent disability/disfigurement, § 784.041) is a 3rd-degree felony.
- **Drug possession — § 893.13(6):** Unauthorized actual or constructive possession of a controlled substance is a 3rd-degree felony; possession of 20 grams or less of cannabis (excluding resin) is a 1st-degree misdemeanor. Sale/manufacture/delivery or possession with intent: 2nd-degree felony for § 893.03(1)(a), (1)(b), (1)(d), (2)(a), (2)(b) drugs; 3rd-degree felony for (1)(c), (2)(c), (3), (4) drugs; 1st-degree misdemeanor for (5) drugs.
- **Habitual felony offender — § 775.084(1)(a), (4)(a):** 2+ prior felony convictions (Florida or qualified out-of-state), current felony committed while serving a sentence/supervision or within 5 years of the last prior conviction or release, whichever is later; the current offense and one prior cannot both be simple § 893.13 purchase/possession. Enhanced maxima: life/1st-degree felony up to life; 2nd-degree up to 30 years; 3rd-degree up to 10 years.
- **Habitual violent felony offender — § 775.084(1)(b), (4)(b):** At least one prior enumerated violent felony; life/1st-degree felony up to life with no release for 15 years; 2nd-degree up to 30 years, no release for 10 years.
- **Three-time violent felony offender / violent career criminal — § 775.084(1)(c)-(d), (4)(c)-(d):** 2+ prior enumerated violent felonies and a current enumerated violent felony; mandatory minimums apply. Violent career criminal: 3+ prior forcible-felony-type convictions plus prior state/federal prison; up to life (1st-degree/life felony), up to 40 years with 30-year mandatory minimum (2nd-degree), up to 15 years with 10-year mandatory minimum (3rd-degree).
- **Criminal traffic vs. noncriminal infractions:** Chapter 318 was adopted to decriminalize certain violations of ch. 316, ch. 320, ch. 322, and others. — § 318.12. "Infraction" = a noncriminal violation, not punishable by incarceration, with no right to a jury trial or court-appointed counsel. — § 318.13(3). Default rule: except as provided in §§ 318.17 and 320.07(3)(c), a person cited for a ch. 316 violation is charged with a noncriminal infraction. — § 318.14(1). Excepted (criminal) offenses: chapter 318 does not apply to fleeing/eluding (§ 316.1935), leaving the scene of a crash (§§ 316.027, 316.061), DUI (§ 316.193), reckless driving (§ 316.192), false crash reports (§ 316.067), willfully failing to obey a lawful police/fire order (§ 316.072(3)), obstructing an officer (§ 316.545(1)), or any other ch. 316 offense classified as a criminal violation. — § 318.17

## 22. Criminal procedure — Fla. R. Crim. P.

- **Arrest and first appearance — Rule 3.130:** Every arrested person not previously lawfully released must be taken before a judge, in person or by audio-video, **within 24 hours of arrest**; state attorney and public defender must attend. At first appearance the judge must immediately inform the defendant of the charge, give a copy of the complaint, and advise: no obligation to speak and anything said may be used against them; right to counsel, appointed if financially unable to afford it; right to communicate with counsel, family, or friends. If the defendant is entitled to appointed counsel and wants it, the judge must appoint counsel immediately, no later than first appearance and before other proceedings. The judge then determines pretrial detention or release under Rule 3.132 or 3.131.
- **Bail / pretrial release — Rule 3.131; Art. I, § 14, Fla. Const.:** "Unless charged with a capital offense or an offense punishable by life imprisonment and the proof of guilt is evident or the presumption is great, every person charged with a crime or violation of municipal or county ordinance shall be entitled to pretrial release on reasonable conditions. If no conditions of release can reasonably protect the community from risk of physical harm to persons, assure the presence of the accused at trial, or assure the integrity of the judicial process, the accused may be detained." Release conditions include refraining from criminal activity and from any contact with the victim except through pretrial discovery. Bail determination must consider nature/circumstances of the offense, weight of evidence, family ties, residence, employment, finances, mental condition, past conduct/flight/failure to appear, danger to the community, source of bail funds, and whether already on release/probation/parole. — § 903.046(2). Since Jan. 1, 2024 the Supreme Court adopts a uniform statewide bond schedule annually; it does not bind a judge at first appearance/bail determination, and certain defendants may not be released before first appearance. — § 903.011; AOSC25-69. Pretrial detention statute: presumption in favor of release on nonmonetary conditions; detention requires substantial probability plus defined grounds. — § 907.041
- **Speedy trial — Rule 3.191:** **90 days for a misdemeanor, 175 days for a felony** — but since the July 1, 2025 amendment, the clock runs from **formal charge** (information/indictment), not arrest. — Rule 3.191(a), as amended in *In re Amendments to Fla. R. Crim. P. 3.191*, No. SC2022-1123 (Fla. Mar. 13, 2025), eff. July 1, 2025. A defendant charged by indictment/information may demand trial within **60 days** by filing a separate pleading entitled "Demand for Speedy Trial" and serving the prosecutor. — Rule 3.191(b). The 2025 amendment also increased the recapture period from 10 to 30 days.
- **Discovery — Rule 3.220:** After the charging document is filed, a defendant may elect discovery by filing/serving a "Notice of Discovery," binding both prosecution and defense to all discovery procedures in the rule. Within **15 days** after service of the Notice of Discovery, the prosecutor must serve a Discovery Exhibit disclosing witness names/addresses, witness statements, the defendant's statements, and other material in the State's possession/control. The prosecutor must disclose as soon as practicable any material information tending to negate guilt, regardless of reciprocal discovery obligations. Either party may take discovery depositions on oral examination after the charging document is filed.
- **Plea types — Rule 3.170:** A defendant may plead **not guilty, guilty, or, with the court's consent, nolo contendere (no contest)**; pleas are in open court and entered by the defendant, except a not-guilty plea may be entered in writing by counsel. A misdemeanor defendant may plead guilty at first appearance under Rule 3.130, and the judge may then enter judgment and sentence without further formal charges. Standing mute or pleading evasively = a not-guilty plea is entered; a not-guilty plea denies every material allegation. Before accepting a guilty or nolo contendere plea, the court must determine in open court, on the record, that the plea reflects full understanding and voluntariness and that there is a factual basis for a guilty plea. — Rule 3.170(k) / 3.172
- **Right to counsel — Rule 3.111; Art. I, § 16, Fla. Const.:** Counsel must be appointed at the earliest of: formal charge, as soon as feasible after custodial restraint, or first appearance. Counsel shall be provided to indigent persons in all prosecutions for offenses punishable by imprisonment, including appeals; no counsel need be provided for a misdemeanor/ordinance violation if the judge files a written order of no incarceration at least 15 days before trial. Florida recognizes an independent state constitutional right to appointed counsel for indigent defendants under Art. I, §§ 2 and 16. — *Traylor v. State*, 596 So. 2d 957, 969–70 (Fla. 1992)

## 23. Civil procedure — Fla. R. Civ. P. and Chs. 47, 48, 95

- **Jurisdiction and venue:**
  - Long-arm (specific jurisdiction): A nonresident who does any listed act "thereby submits" to Florida jurisdiction for a cause of action arising from it, including operating a business in Florida, "[c]ommitting a tortious act within this state," owning real property in Florida, and breaching a contract in Florida. — § 48.193(1)(a)
  - Long-arm (general jurisdiction): "A defendant who is engaged in substantial and not isolated activity within this state... is subject to the jurisdiction of the courts of this state, whether or not the claim arises from that activity." — § 48.193(2)
  - Venue, general rule: "Actions shall be brought only in the county where the defendant resides, where the cause of action accrued, or where the property in litigation is located. This section shall not apply to actions against nonresidents." — § 47.011
  - Venue, multiple defendants / corporations: Actions against two or more defendants in different counties may be brought in any county where any defendant resides (§ 47.021); domestic corporations are sued where they keep an office for customary business, where the cause accrued, or where the property is located (§ 47.051).
- **Service of process (Ch. 48):**
  - Who serves: "All process shall be served by the sheriff of the county where the person to be served is found," except nonenforceable civil process and certain subpoenas/summonses may be served by a special or certified process server. — § 48.021
  - How served: "Service of original process is made by delivering a copy of it to the person to be served with a copy of the complaint... or by leaving the copies at his or her usual place of abode with any person residing therein who is 15 years of age or older and informing the person of their contents." — § 48.031(1)(a)
  - Substituted service on nonresidents: After due diligence, service may be made through the Secretary of State, with notice and a copy also sent to the party and an affidavit of compliance filed within 40 days. — § 48.161
  - Return of service: The server must note date/time of receipt and service, manner, person served, and all documents served, and sign the return; failure to state the facts or sign "invalidates the service, but the return is amendable." — § 48.21
- **Pleadings and motions:**
  - Pleadings allowed: "There shall be a complaint... and an answer to it; an answer to a counterclaim...; an answer to a cross-claim...; a third party complaint... and a third party answer... No other pleadings shall be allowed." — Rule 1.100(a)
  - Answer deadline and motion to dismiss: "A defendant shall serve his answer within 20 days after service of original process and the initial pleading" (Rule 1.140(a)); seven defenses may be raised by motion instead, including lack of subject-matter or personal jurisdiction, improper venue, insufficiency of process/service, failure to state a cause of action, and failure to join an indispensable party (Rule 1.140(b)).
- **Discovery (Rules 1.280–1.390):**
  - Initial disclosures: A party must, "without awaiting a discovery request," provide initial disclosures "within 60 days after the service of the complaint or joinder, unless a different time is set by court order." — Rule 1.280(a)(1), (a)(3)
  - Methods: Discovery is by "depositions on oral examination or written questions; written interrogatories; production of documents or things or permission to enter upon land...; physical and mental examinations; and requests for admission." — Rule 1.280(b)
  - Rule map: 1.310 depositions on oral examination; 1.340 interrogatories; 1.350 production/entry on land; 1.360 examination of persons; 1.370 requests for admission; 1.380 failure to make discovery/sanctions; 1.390 depositions of expert witnesses.
- **Summary judgment (Rule 1.510):** "The court shall grant summary judgment if the movant shows that there is no genuine dispute as to any material fact and the movant is entitled to judgment as a matter of law." The standard "shall be construed and applied in accordance with the federal summary judgment standard" (Celotex trilogy), effective May 1, 2021. — Rule 1.510(a). Timing caveat: The 2021 rule required the motion at least 40 days before hearing and the response at least 20 days before; the Jan. 1, 2025 amendments revised Rule 1.510 timing, so verify current timing in the current rule text before relying on the 40/20 figures.
- **Statutes of limitations — § 95.11:** Written contract — 5 years (§ 95.11(2)(b)). Negligence — **2 years** (§ 95.11(4)(a); was 4 years before the 2023 tort reform). Wrongful death — 2 years (§ 95.11(4)(e)).

## 24. Personal injury and negligence

- **Negligence elements:** (1) duty — defendant owed a legal duty of care; (2) breach — failure to conform to that standard; (3) causation — breach was the legal cause of injury; (4) damages — actual harm/loss. Fla. Std. Jury Instr. (Civ.) 401.3. "Negligence is the failure to use reasonable care," i.e. doing something a reasonably careful person would not do, or failing to do something they would do, under like circumstances. Fla. Std. Jury Instr. (Civ.) 401.3/401.4.
- **Comparative fault — § 768.81:** "Contributory fault chargeable to the claimant diminishes proportionately the amount awarded as economic and noneconomic damages," subject to subsection (6). — § 768.81(2). **51% bar (2023 amendment):** "Any party found to be greater than 50 percent at fault for his or her own harm may not recover any damages." — § 768.81(6). A claimant at exactly 50% is not barred by this text. Medical-malpractice exception: subsection (6) "does not apply to an action for damages for personal injury or wrongful death arising out of medical negligence pursuant to chapter 766." No joint and several liability: "The court shall enter judgment against each party liable on the basis of such party's percentage of fault and not on the basis of the doctrine of joint and several liability." — § 768.81(3). Nonparty fault: A defendant must affirmatively plead a nonparty's fault and prove it at trial by a preponderance of the evidence to get the nonparty on the verdict form. — § 768.81(3)(a). Subsection (6) added by s. 9, ch. 2023-15 (HB 837).
- **Damages review — § 768.74 (not a numeric cap):** Where liability is found and a money verdict is rendered, the court, upon proper motion, must review the award to determine if it is "excessive or inadequate in light of the facts and circumstances" presented to the trier of fact. — § 768.74(1). If excessive or inadequate, the court "shall order a remittitur or additur, as the case may be." — § 768.74(2). "If the party adversely affected by such remittitur or additur does not agree, the court shall order a new trial in the cause on the issue of damages only." — § 768.74(4). Criteria: prejudice/passion/corruption, whether the trier ignored the evidence or misconceived the merits, improper elements of damages, speculation/conjecture, reasonable relation to damages proved, and whether the award is supported by the evidence. — § 768.74(5). § 768.74 sets a review standard, not a fixed dollar cap on compensatory damages.
- **Wrongful death — §§ 768.16–768.26:** "Sections 768.16–768.26 may be cited as the 'Florida Wrongful Death Act.'" — § 768.16. Public policy is "to shift the losses resulting when wrongful death occurs from the survivors of the decedent to the wrongdoer"; the Act is remedial and "shall be liberally construed." — § 768.17. "Survivors" means the decedent's spouse, children, parents, and, when partly or wholly dependent for support or services, any blood relatives and adoptive brothers and sisters. — § 768.18(1). Each survivor may recover lost support and services (past, with interest; future, reduced to present value). — § 768.21(1). Surviving spouse may recover loss of companionship/protection and mental pain and suffering. — § 768.21(2). Minor children (and all children if no surviving spouse) may recover lost parental companionship, instruction, guidance, and mental pain and suffering. — § 768.21(3). Each parent of a deceased minor child may recover mental pain and suffering; each parent of an adult child may do so only if there are no other survivors. — § 768.21(4). The personal representative may recover the decedent's lost earnings from injury to death (less lost support of survivors, with interest), prospective net accumulations where applicable, and medical/funeral expenses charged to the estate. — § 768.21(6). Medical-negligence limit: § 768.21(3) damages are not recoverable by adult children, and § 768.21(4) damages are not recoverable by parents of an adult child, for medical negligence as defined in § 766.106(1). — § 768.21(8). "The amounts awarded to each survivor and to the estate shall be stated separately in the verdict." — § 768.22
- **Premises liability — § 768.0755:** Slip-and-fall on a transitory foreign substance in a business establishment: The injured person "must prove that the business establishment had actual or constructive knowledge of the dangerous condition and should have taken action to remedy it." — § 768.0755(1). Constructive knowledge is provable by circumstantial evidence showing (a) the condition existed long enough that, in ordinary care, the business should have known of it, or (b) the condition occurred with regularity and was therefore foreseeable. — § 768.0755(1)(a)–(b). "This section does not affect any common-law duty of care owed by a person or entity in possession or control of a business premises." — § 768.0755(2)
- **Statute of limitations — § 95.11:** Negligence: 2 years. — § 95.11(5)(a) (note: the 2-year negligence period reflects the 2023 amendment (s. 3, ch. 2023-15); it was 4 years before March 24, 2023). Wrongful death: 2 years. — § 95.11(5)(e). Medical malpractice: 2 years from incident or discovery, capped at 4 years from the incident, except for a minor on or before the child's eighth birthday; fraud/concealment extends discovery by 2 years, up to 7 years. — § 95.11(5)(c)

X, § 4(a) — Exemption from forced sale: Homestead owned by a natural person is exempt from forced sale under process of any court, and no judgment/decree/execution is a lien on it, except for taxes/assessments, obligations for purchase/improvement/repair, or house/field/other labor on the realty. Limit: 160 acres contiguous outside a municipality; 1/2 acre contiguous inside a municipality, limited to the owner's/family's residence; plus $1,000 personal property.
  - Art. X, § 4(b)–(c) — Survivors; alienation/devise limits: Exemptions inure to the surviving spouse or heirs; homestead is not devisable if survived by spouse or minor child, except it may be devised to the spouse if no minor child; the owner, joined by the spouse if married, may alienate it by mortgage, sale, or gift.

  - § 95.16 — Under color of title: Adverse possession under color of title (written instrument/judgment/decree) is a distinct 7-year route.
  - § 95.18 — Without color of title: Requires actual continued possession of real property for 7 years under a claim of title exclusive of any other right, not founded on a written instrument, judgment, or decree.
- **Easements — Ch. 704:**
  - § 704.01(1) — Implied grant of way of necessity: Recognizes/adopts the common-law implied grant where granted/retained land has no accessible right-of-way except over the grantor's land and no other reasonable/practicable way of egress/ingress exists.
  - § 704.01(2) — Statutory way of necessity: A separate statutory way exists for land outside a municipality used/desired for dwellings, agriculture, timber, or stockraising that is shut off/hemmed in with no practicable route to the nearest public/private road; the owner/tenant may use/maintain an easement for persons, vehicles, stock, cable, and utilities by the nearest practical route, in an orderly/proper manner, without it constituting trespass.
  - §§ 704.04, 704.06, 704.07 — Remedy and special easements: § 704.04 provides judicial remedy/compensation to the servient owner; § 704.06 covers conservation easements; § 704.07 covers solar easements.
- **Landlord/tenant overlap — Ch. 83, Pt. II:** § 83.51 (landlord's maintenance duty); § 83.56 (termination of rental agreement — 7-day written-notice/cure process); § 83.59 (right of action for possession — only by court action, surrender, or abandonment, not self-help); § 83.5615 (Protecting Tenants at Foreclosure Act).
- **Foreclosure basics — Ch. 702:**
  - § 702.01 — Equity: All mortgages shall be foreclosed in equity; counterclaims against the foreclosing mortgagee are severed for separate trial, and the foreclosure claim, if tried, is tried to the court without a jury.
  - § 702.07 — Setting aside decree before sale: Circuit courts may rescind/vacate/set aside a foreclosure decree at any time before the sale is actually made, and dismiss the proceeding on payment of court costs.
  - § 702.10 — Order to show cause / expedited foreclosure: A lienholder may seek an order to show cause for entry of final judgment; the hearing may not occur sooner than the later of 20 days after service of the order or 45 days after service of the initial complaint; the court may enter final judgment and order the clerk to conduct a foreclosure sale.

## 26. Landlord-tenant — Ch. 83

- **Structure:** Ch. 83 has three parts: Part I, Nonresidential Tenancies (§§ 83.001–83.251); Part II, Residential Tenancies (§§ 83.40–83.684); Part III, Self-Service Storage (§§ 83.801–83.809). § 83.40: Part II is the "Florida Residential Landlord and Tenant Act."
- **Security deposits — § 83.49:**
  - Landlord must hold deposit/advance rent (beyond next period) in one of three ways: (a) separate non-interest-bearing account in a Florida financial institution; (b) separate interest-bearing account, tenant receiving at least 75% of the annualized average interest rate or 5% simple interest per year, as landlord elects; or (c) surety bond with the circuit court clerk, capped at $50,000 (or $250,000 statewide for landlords in 5+ counties), plus 5% simple interest to tenant. No commingling.
  - Landlord must give written notice in the lease or within 30 days of receipt, stating depository/bond, interest entitlement, and the statutory disclosure; notice in person, mail, or e-mail per § 83.505. Small landlords (<5 units) exempt from this notice subsection.
  - If no claim: return deposit + interest within 15 days after termination. If claiming: written notice by certified mail/e-mail within 30 days after termination, stating amount and reason; tenant must object in writing within 15 days of receipt; landlord then deducts claim and remits balance within 30 days after the claim notice. Failure to give timely claim notice forfeits the right to impose a claim/setoff against the deposit (landlord may still sue for damages after returning it).
  - Prevailing party in a deposit action gets court costs plus reasonable attorney's fee. — § 83.49(3)(c)
  - Tenant vacating/abandoning early must give at least 7 days' written notice by certified mail or personal delivery, including a forwarding address; failure relieves landlord of the § 83.49(3)(a) notice duty but does not waive tenant's deposit rights. — § 83.49(5)
  - § 83.491 (related): landlord may offer a fee in lieu of a security deposit, or monthly installments.
- **Landlord maintenance — § 83.51:** Landlord must at all times: comply with applicable building, housing, and health codes; or, where none apply, keep roofs, windows, doors, floors, steps, porches, exterior walls, foundations, and other structural components in good repair and plumbing in reasonable working condition. Screens must be installed in reasonable condition at commencement and repaired once annually when necessary. Duties under § 83.51(1) may be altered/modified in writing for a single-family home or duplex. For dwellings other than single-family/duplex, landlord must also make reasonable provisions for: extermination of rats, mice, roaches, ants, wood-destroying organisms, and bedbugs; locks and keys; clean and safe common areas; garbage removal/receptacles; and functioning heat, running water, and hot water. Working smoke detectors must be installed at commencement of a single-family/duplex tenancy. Noncompliance with § 83.51(2) alone is not a defense to a possession action under § 83.59. — § 83.51(2)(c)
- **Tenant duties — § 83.52:** "Tenant's obligation to maintain dwelling unit." Its breach matters procedurally: under § 83.56(2), a tenant's material failure to comply with § 83.52 (other than nonpayment) is a ground for landlord termination.
- **Termination — §§ 83.56, 83.57, 83.575:**
  - § 83.56(1): if landlord materially fails to comply with § 83.51(1) or material lease provisions, tenant may terminate after 7 days' written notice specifying noncompliance and intent to terminate (unless failure is beyond landlord's control and landlord is making every reasonable effort to correct it).
  - § 83.56(2): if tenant materially fails to comply with § 83.52, material lease provisions, or reasonable rules (other than nonpayment), landlord may terminate; curable violations get a cure opportunity, non-curable or repeat violations within 12 months of a written warning do not.
  - § 83.56(3) — 3-day notice for nonpayment: if rent default continues for 3 days, excluding Saturday, Sunday, and legal holidays (court-observed holidays only), after written demand for payment or possession, landlord may terminate. The notice must follow the statutory form demanding rent or possession within 3 days.
  - § 83.56(4): notices under (1)–(3) by mail, delivery, e-mail per § 83.505, or leaving a copy at the residence if tenant is absent; these notice requirements may not be waived in the rental agreement.
  - § 83.57 governs termination of a tenancy without a specific term; § 83.575 governs termination of a tenancy with a specific duration.
- **Eviction / possession — §§ 83.59–83.62:**
  - § 83.59, "Right of action for possession": if the rental agreement is terminated and the tenant does not vacate, the landlord may recover possession as provided in that section.
  - § 83.60, "Defenses to action for rent or possession": in a nonpayment possession action (or § 83.55 rent action), tenant may defend on material noncompliance with § 83.51(1) or raise any other legal or equitable defense.
  - § 83.62, "Restoration of possession to landlord": after judgment for the landlord, the clerk issues a writ to the sheriff commanding the sheriff to put the landlord in possession after 24 hours' notice.
  - Related: § 83.595 (choice of remedies on breach/early termination), § 83.61 (disbursement of court-registry funds; prompt final hearing), § 83.67 (prohibited practices).
- **Nonresidential tenancies — Part I, §§ 83.001–83.20:** § 83.001: Part I applies to nonresidential tenancies and all tenancies not governed by Part II. § 83.03: a tenancy at will may be terminated by either party on notice as specified in that section. § 83.05: if a person leasing/renting land or premises other than a dwelling unit fails to pay rent when due, the lessor has the right to obtain possession as provided by law. § 83.06: landlord may demand double rent on refusal to deliver possession; § 83.08: landlord's lien for rent; § 83.20: causes for removal of tenants; § 83.201: tenant notice of failure to maintain/repair rendering premises wholly untenantable, with right to withhold rent.

## 27. Family law

- **Dissolution of marriage — Ch. 61:**
  - § 61.021 — Residency: one party must reside in Florida 6 months before filing the petition. § 61.052 — Grounds: only (a) marriage irretrievably broken, or (b) mental incapacity adjudged under § 744.331 for at least 3 years.
  - § 61.075 — Equitable distribution: court sets apart each spouse's nonmarital assets/liabilities, then distributes marital assets/liabilities starting from a premise of equal distribution, unless justified unequal by all relevant factors, including contributions as homemaker/parent, economic circumstances, duration, career interruption, and dissipation within 2 years before filing. Court first finds actual need and ability to pay, then weighs factors including duration, standard of living, age/physical/mental/emotional condition, and resources. - **Children — § 61.13, § 61.30:**
  - § 61.13 — Parenting/time-sharing: court determines parenting/time-sharing by best interests of the child and the UCCJEA. A court-approved parenting plan must, at minimum, describe daily tasks, the time-sharing schedule, health-care/school/activity responsibility, and parent-child communication methods. Modification requires a substantial, material, and unanticipated change of circumstances.
  - § 61.30 — Child support guidelines: the guideline amount presumptively establishes the amount ordered, in initial or modification proceedings under this or another chapter, for a minor child or a dependent 18-19-year-old still in high school in good faith expecting graduation before 19. The trier of fact may vary ±5% after considering relevant factors; a larger variance requires a written finding that the guideline amount would be unjust or inappropriate. Guidelines can prove substantial change for modification only if the difference is at least 15% or $50, whichever is greater. Gross income includes wages, bonuses/commissions/overtime/tips, business income, disability, workers' comp, and reemployment/unemployment compensation; income is imputed to a voluntarily unemployed/underemployed parent absent incapacity or circumstances beyond control. Net income is gross minus allowable deductions; parents' net incomes are combined and the § 61.30(6) schedule applied.
- **Paternity — Ch. 742:**
  - § 742.10 — Establishment for children born out of wedlock: Ch. 742 provides the primary jurisdiction/procedures. Paternity is established by adjudication, affidavit/stipulation filed with the clerk, notarized or two-witness voluntary acknowledgment signed under penalty of perjury under §§ 382.013/382.016, or Department of Revenue adjudication under § 409.256. Without an adjudicatory proceeding, a voluntary acknowledgment creates a rebuttable presumption and may be rescinded within 60 days after signing or after an administrative/judicial proceeding relating to the child, whichever is earlier; after 60 days it may be challenged only on fraud, duress, or material mistake of fact, with the burden on the challenger.
- **Domestic violence injunctions — Ch. 741:**
  - § 741.30 — Injunction for protection: creates a cause of action in circuit court. Standing: a family/household member who is a victim of domestic violence as defined in § 741.28, or has reasonable cause to believe they are in imminent danger of becoming a victim. No filing fee, no bond, no attorney required, no mutual orders of protection; a petition may be filed where petitioner currently/temporarily resides, respondent resides, or violence occurred. The court is prohibited from issuing mutual orders, but may issue separate injunctions if each party complies. Chapter 61 orders entered later take precedence over inconsistent injunction provisions governed by Ch. 61. Clerk must assist petitioners.
- **Dependency — Ch. 39:**
  - § 39.01 — Definitions: "child who is found to be dependent" includes a child abandoned, abused, or neglected by a parent/legal custodian, or at substantial risk of imminent abuse, abandonment, or neglect. "Abuse" is a willful act/threatened act causing or likely to cause significant impairment of physical, mental, or emotional health.
  - § 39.507 — Adjudicatory hearings: hearing by judge without jury, civil evidence rules, no later than 30 days after arraignment; dependency must be proved by a preponderance of the evidence; anonymous-call evidence must be independently corroborated and alone cannot support adjudication; if the child is not dependent the court shall dismiss the case; only one order adjudicating each child dependent is entered while the court maintains jurisdiction.
  - §§ 39.806/39.809 — Termination of parental rights: a statutory ground under § 39.806 must be proved by clear and convincing evidence under § 39.809(1), and the court must also consider manifest best interests under § 39.810.
- **Florida Courts forms/rules:** Parenting Plan, Form 12.995(a): required in all cases involving time-sharing with minor child(ren), even when not in dispute; Form 12.995(b) is the supervised/safety-focused plan. Paternity/support forms: Form 12.983(a) is the Petition to Determine Paternity and for Related Relief; with it file UCCJEA Affidavit 12.902(d), financial affidavit 12.902(b)/(c), Child Support Guidelines Worksheet 12.902(e), and Parenting Plan 12.995(a)/(b)/(c).

## 28. Probate and estate — Chs. 732, 733, 735, 736

- **Wills — Ch. 732:**
  - § 732.502 — Execution: Every will must be in writing; testator signs at the end (or another person subscribes the name in the testator's presence and by direction); signing/acknowledgment must be before at least two attesting witnesses, who sign in the testator's presence and each other's presence. A codicil needs the same formalities. Holographic/nuncupative wills by nonresidents are not validated by foreign law alone.
  - Ch. 732 is "Probate Code: Intestate Succession and Wills," Part I intestate succession §§ 732.101–732.111, Part II elective share §§ 732.201–732.228.
- **Intestate succession — §§ 732.101–732.111:**
  - § 732.101: Any part of an estate not effectively disposed of by will passes to heirs as prescribed in Part I; death vests heirs' rights in intestate property.
  - § 732.102, spouse's share: If no surviving descendant, spouse takes the entire intestate estate; if all surviving descendants are also the spouse's lineal descendants, spouse takes first $60,000 plus one-half of the balance.
  - § 732.103, other heirs: The part not passing to the spouse (or the whole estate if no spouse) descends to descendants, then parents, then siblings/collateral heirs in the order stated.
  - § 732.104: Descent is per stirpes, to descendants or collateral heirs.
- **Probate administration — Ch. 733:**
  - § 733.101, venue: Probate venue is the county where the decedent was domiciled.
  - § 733.103: Until admitted to probate in Florida (or the domicile state), a will is ineffective to prove title to or right to possession of the testator's property.
  - § 733.202, petition: A verified petition for administration may be filed by any interested person, stating petitioner's interest, decedent's identity/domicile/date and place of death, beneficiaries, and venue; testate petitions must identify unrevoked wills/codicils and show the original will is with the court or accompanies the petition.
  - § 733.212, notice of administration: Personal representative must promptly serve notice on surviving spouse, beneficiaries, and persons entitled to exempt property; objections to will validity, venue, or jurisdiction must be filed within 3 months after service, or are forever barred.
  - § 733.2121, notice to creditors: Personal representative must promptly publish notice to creditors unless claims are otherwise barred by § 733.710.
  - § 733.603: Personal representative proceeds expeditiously with settlement/distribution, generally without court order/adjudication unless the code requires it or the court orders otherwise.
  - Florida Probate Rules: Rule 5.200 governs the petition for administration (verified, with decedent's last 4 SSN digits, domicile, beneficiaries, and PR priority/qualifications); Rules 5.240 (notice of administration), 5.405 (protected homestead proceedings), and 5.530 (summary administration) are the companion procedure rules.
- **Summary administration — Ch. 735:**
  - § 735.201: Summary administration is available for resident or nonresident decedents when (1) in a testate estate the will does not direct administration under Ch. 733, and (2) the Florida estate subject to administration, less property exempt from creditors' claims, does not exceed $75,000, **or** the decedent has been dead more than 2 years. §§ 735.201–735.2063 govern the proceeding; no personal representative is appointed — the court enters an order distributing assets.
  - Caveat: Secondary sources in search results claimed Ch. 2026-57 raised the $75,000 figure to $150,000 effective July 1, 2026 — that was **not** confirmed from a primary source here and is not asserted as fact.
- **Elective share — §§ 732.201, 732.2065:**
  - § 732.201: The surviving spouse of a person dying domiciled in Florida has the right to a share of the elective estate, designated the elective share; the election does not reduce what the spouse receives if no election were made.
  - § 732.2065: "The elective share is an amount equal to 30 percent of the elective estate."
- **Homestead in probate:**
  - § 732.401 / § 732.4015: Descent of protected homestead is governed by § 732.401; devise of homestead is restricted by § 732.4015 (homestead cannot be devised away from a surviving spouse/minor child except as the section permits). Full operative text was **not** verified from primary text in this run.
  - Fla. Prob. R. 5.405 governs proceedings to determine protected homestead status of real property.
  - Homestead life-estate / one-half election detail (spouse takes life estate with remainder to descendants, or may elect a one-half tenancy-in-common interest within 6 months) appeared only in law-firm summaries in search results, so it is omitted pending a primary-text check of § 732.401.
- **Trusts — Ch. 736 (Florida Trust Code):**
  - § 736.0101 / § 736.0102: Ch. 736 is the "Florida Trust Code"; it applies to express trusts (charitable/noncharitable) and trusts created by law, judgment, or decree to be administered as express trusts.
  - § 736.0401, methods of creation: By transfer of property to another as trustee during life or by will/disposition at death; by declaration that the owner holds identifiable property as trustee; or by exercise of a power of appointment in favor of a trustee.
  - § 736.0402, requirements: A trust is created only if the settlor has capacity, indicates intent to create it, there is a definite beneficiary (or a charitable, animal-care, or noncharitable-purpose trust), the trustee has duties to perform, and the same person is not sole trustee and sole beneficiary.
  - § 736.0601 / § 736.0602, revocable trusts: Capacity to create/amend/revoke a revocable trust is the same as to make a will; unless the trust expressly provides it is irrevocable, the settlor may revoke or amend it.

## 29. Business and corporate

- **LLCs — Ch. 605, Florida Revised LLC Act:**
  - § 605.0101: Ch. 605 may be cited as the "Florida Revised Limited Liability Company Act."
  - § 605.0108: An LLC is an entity distinct from its members; it may have any lawful purpose; it has indefinite duration.
  - § 605.0201: One or more persons may form an LLC by signing and delivering articles of organization to the Department for filing.
  - § 605.0112: LLC name must contain "limited liability company," "L.L.C." or "LLC" to clearly indicate it is an LLC.
  - § 605.0113: Each LLC and each foreign LLC with a certificate of authority shall designate and continuously maintain in Florida a registered office and registered agent.
  - § 605.0714: Department may administratively dissolve an LLC if it does not deliver its annual report by 5:00 p.m. ET on the third Friday in September of each year.
  - § 605.0902: A foreign LLC may not transact business in Florida until it obtains a certificate of authority from the Department.
- **Corporations — Ch. 607, Florida Business Corporation Act:**
  - § 607.0101: Ch. 607 may be cited as the "Florida Business Corporation Act"; Part I is general, Part II social purpose corporations, Part III benefit corporations.
  - § 607.0202 / § 607.0203: Incorporation is by articles of incorporation; content and filing are in those sections.
  - § 607.0301–§ 607.0302: Every corporation has the purpose of engaging in any lawful business unless articles limit it; unless articles provide otherwise, it has perpetual duration and the same powers as an individual to do things necessary/convenient to carry out its business.
  - § 607.0501 / § 607.0505: Each corporation / foreign corporation transacting business in Florida shall have and continuously maintain a registered office and registered agent in Florida; agent must file acceptance.
  - § 607.1622: Each domestic and foreign corporation authorized in Florida shall deliver an annual report to the Department; first report Jan. 1–May 1 of the year following incorporation/qualification, subsequent reports Jan. 1–May 1 each year; failure to file a compliant report bars prosecuting/maintaining an action in Florida courts until filed and fees/penalties paid, and subjects it to dissolution/cancellation.
- **Partnerships — Ch. 620:**
  - § 620.8101(5): "Partnership" means an association of two or more persons to carry on as co-owners a business for profit formed under § 620.8202, predecessor law, or comparable law of another jurisdiction.
  - § 620.8202: Association of two or more persons to carry on as co-owners a business for profit forms a partnership, whether or not the persons intend to form one.
  - § 620.8401: Each partner has an equal share of profits / share of losses in proportion to profits, equal rights in management, and a person may become a partner only with consent of all partners.
  - § 620.1404: General partners are liable jointly and severally for all obligations of the limited partnership, except as provided in subsections (2)–(3).
  - § 620.9001: A partnership may become a limited liability partnership by filing a statement of qualification electing LLP status, with name, chief executive / principal office address, and agent for service of process.
- **Fictitious names — § 865.09, Fictitious Name Act:**
  - § 865.09(2)(c): "Fictitious name" means any name under which a person transacts business in Florida, other than the person's legal name.
  - § 865.09(3)(a): A person may not engage in business under a fictitious name unless first registered with the Division of Corporations, listing the name, mailing address, name/address of each registrant, and certification that intent to register was advertised at least once in a newspaper (Ch. 50) in the county of the principal place of business.
  - § 865.09(5): Registration is valid from registration/reregistration until Dec. 31 of the 5th calendar year thereafter, counting the registration year as the first calendar year; renewals are 5 years.
  - § 865.09(4): If a registrant ceases business under the name, file a cancellation with the Division within 30 days.
- **Annual reports and Sunbiz filings:** Annual report updates/confirms Division records; it is not a financial statement, and must be filed each year to maintain active status. — dos.fl.gov/sunbiz
- **Registered agents:** Registered agent is the individual or legal entity designated to accept service of process; a business entity cannot serve as its own registered agent, but an individual/principal associated with the business may; agent must have a physical Florida street address, not a P.O. Box; a new agent must sign, confirming familiarity with Florida Statutes and accepting the obligations. Statutory anchors: § 605.0113 (LLCs), § 607.0501/§ 607.0505 (corporations).
- **Piercing the corporate veil — basics / gap:** No Ch. 605/607 section found in the primary text reviewed codifies "piercing the veil." The statutory shield is structural: § 605.0108 (LLC distinct from members) and the Ch. 607 corporate form. Veil-piercing is a judicial equitable doctrine, not a Sunbiz filing rule — do not cite a statute section for its 3-part test.

## 30. Contracts

- **Formation:** No single Florida statute codifies general offer, acceptance, and consideration. Those elements are common law; the statutes codify formation only for sales of goods.
- **§ 672.204 — Formation in general (goods):** A contract for sale of goods "may be made in any manner sufficient to show agreement, including conduct by both parties which recognizes the existence of such a contract"; the moment of making may be undetermined; open terms do not defeat the contract if the parties intended to contract and there is "a reasonably certain basis for giving an appropriate remedy."
- **§ 672.206 — Offer and acceptance in formation of contract; § 672.205 — Firm offers; § 672.209 — Modification, rescission, and waiver:** Listed in ch. 672, Part II (ss. 672.201–672.210).
- **§ 672.207 — Additional terms in acceptance or confirmation:** A "definite and seasonable expression of acceptance" operates as acceptance even with additional or different terms, "unless acceptance is expressly made conditional on assent to the additional or different terms"; between merchants additional terms become part of the contract subject to the stated limits; conduct recognizing a contract suffices even if the writings do not.
- **Statute of Frauds — § 725.01:** "No action shall be brought" unless the agreement/promise, "or some note or memorandum thereof shall be in writing and signed by the party to be charged therewith or by some other person by her or him thereunto lawfully authorized," for: (a) an executor/administrator's special promise to pay a debt/damages from their own estate; (b) a special promise to answer for the debt, default, or miscarriage of another; (c) an agreement made upon consideration of marriage; (d) a contract for sale of lands, tenements, hereditaments, or any uncertain interest in them; (e) a lease thereof for longer than 1 year; (f) any agreement "not to be performed within the space of 1 year from the making thereof"; (g) a health care provider's guarantee/warranty/assurance as to results of a medical, surgical, or diagnostic procedure by a ch. 458/459/460/461/466 licensee.
- **UCC Sales — Ch. 672:**
  - § 672.101 — Short title: ch. 672 "shall be known and may be cited as the 'Uniform Commercial Code—Sales.'"
  - § 672.102 — Scope: ch. 672 "applies to transactions in goods," with a predominance test for hybrid transactions (goods + services/lease/other property).
  - § 672.201 — Formal requirements; statute of frauds (goods): A contract for sale of goods "for the price of $500 or more is not enforceable by way of action or defense unless there is a record sufficient to indicate that a contract for sale has been made between the parties and signed by the party against whom enforcement is sought" or their authorized agent/broker; not enforceable "beyond the quantity of goods shown in the record." Between merchants, a timely confirmation record binds the recipient who has reason to know its contents unless objection in a record is given within 10 days. Exceptions in § 672.201(3): specially manufactured goods (substantial beginning/commitments before repudiation notice), admission in pleading/testimony/in court (limited to quantity admitted), and goods for which payment has been made and accepted or which have been received and accepted.
- **Breach and remedies — Ch. 672, Parts VI–VII:**
  - § 672.601 — Buyer's rights on improper delivery: if goods/tender fail "in any respect to conform to the contract," the buyer may reject the whole, accept the whole, or accept any commercial unit(s) and reject the rest (subject to installment-contract and remedy-limitation provisions).
  - Seller remedies, §§ 672.703–672.710: § 672.703 (seller's remedies in general), § 672.706 (resale), § 672.708 (damages for nonacceptance/repudiation), § 672.709 (action for the price), § 672.710 (incidental damages).
  - § 672.708 — Seller's damages: measure is "the difference between the market price at the time and place for tender and the unpaid contract price together with any incidental damages" (§ 672.710), "less expenses saved in consequence of the buyer's breach"; if that is inadequate to put the seller in as good a position as performance would have, the measure is the profit (including reasonable overhead) from full performance, plus incidental damages, with allowance for costs incurred and credit for payments/resale proceeds.
  - Buyer remedies, §§ 672.711–672.716: § 672.711 (buyer's remedies in general; security interest in rejected goods), § 672.712 ("Cover" — procurement of substitute goods), § 672.713 (damages for nondelivery/repudiation), § 672.714 (damages for breach as to accepted goods), § 672.715 (incidental and consequential damages), § 672.716 (specific performance or replevin).
- **Liquidated damages:**
  - § 672.718 — Liquidation or limitation of damages; deposits: "Damages for breach by either party may be liquidated in the agreement but only at an amount which is reasonable in the light of the anticipated or actual harm caused by the breach, the difficulties of proof of loss, and the inconvenience or nonfeasibility of otherwise obtaining an adequate remedy."
  - § 672.719 — Contractual modification or limitation of remedy: parties may modify/limit remedies by agreement, as listed in ch. 672, Part VII (ss. 672.701–672.724).
- **Statute of limitations — § 95.11:**
  - § 95.11(2)(b) — WITHIN FIVE YEARS: "A legal or equitable action on a contract, obligation, or liability founded on a written instrument," except payment-bond claims and deficiency-judgment actions under ¶ (6)(g).
  - § 95.11(3)(j) — WITHIN FOUR YEARS: "A legal or equitable action on a contract, obligation, or liability not founded on a written instrument, including an action for the sale and delivery of goods, wares, and merchandise, and on store accounts."
  - § 95.11(6)(a) — WITHIN ONE YEAR: "An action for specific performance of a contract."
  - Important gap: the opened ch. 672 lists Part VII Remedies as ss. 672.701–672.724, with no § 672.725; Florida ch. 672 therefore has no separate UCC 4-year sales limitations section, so goods-sale claims fall under § 95.11 above (5 years if founded on a written instrument, 4 years if not).

## 31. Consumer protection

- **FDUTPA — Ch. 501, Part II, §§ 501.201–501.213:**
  - Scope/purpose: FDUTPA is Ch. 501, Part II, F.S., the Florida Deceptive and Unfair Trade Practices Act. Its stated purpose is to protect the consuming public and legitimate business enterprises from unfair methods of competition, or unconscionable, deceptive, or unfair acts or practices in trade or commerce — § 501.202.
  - Core ban: "Unfair methods of competition, unconscionable acts or practices, and unfair or deceptive acts or practices in the conduct of any trade or commerce are hereby declared unlawful" — § 501.204(1).
  - Construction: In construing § 501.204(1), "due consideration and great weight shall be given to the interpretations of the Federal Trade Commission and the federal courts relating to s. 5(a)(1) of the Federal Trade Commission Act, 15 U.S.C. s. 45(a)(1)" — § 501.204(2).
  - Part II contents (2024): §§ 501.202, 501.204, 501.2041, 501.2042, 501.206, 501.2065, 501.207, 501.2077, 501.2079, 501.208, 501.2101, et seq.
  - Enforcement: The Attorney General is the FDUTPA enforcing authority and brings actions for injunctive relief, restitution, civil penalties, and attorney's fees/costs under Ch. 501, Part II.
  - **Not fully verified:** The Part II list includes § 501.2105 "Attorney's fees" and § 501.211 "Other individual remedies," and § 501.207 "Remedies of enforcing authority" — but the current text, damages measure, and fee-shifting rule were not opened successfully in this run (429), so amounts and the prevailing-party rule are not stated here.
- **Telemarketing / robocalls — § 501.059:**
  - "Telephonic sales call" includes calls, texts, and voicemail transmissions to a consumer to solicit a sale, solicit credit, or obtain information for direct solicitation — § 501.059(1)(j).
  - Identification: A solicitor making an unsolicited telephonic sales call must identify himself/herself by true first and last names and the business on whose behalf he/she is soliciting immediately upon contact — § 501.059(2).
  - Do-not-call list: A subscriber may notify the Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services to be placed on the "no sales solicitation calls" listing; solicitors may not make unsolicited calls to numbers on the then-current quarterly listing — § 501.059(3)–(4).
  - Prior opt-out: A solicitor may not initiate an outbound call, text, or voicemail to a consumer/business/donor who previously said they do not wish to receive them — § 501.059(5).
  - Robocall / autodialer consent: A person may not make or knowingly allow an unsolicited telephonic sales call involving an automated system for selection/dialing or playing a recorded message when connected, without the called party's prior express written consent — § 501.059(8)(a). "Prior express written consent" requires a signed written agreement clearly authorizing such calls/texts/voicemails, including the number and a clear disclosure that signing is not a condition of purchase — § 501.059(1)(g).
  - Caller ID / spoofing / voice alteration: It is unlawful to fail to transmit the originating number (and solicitor name when available), and unlawful to intentionally alter the caller's voice to disguise identity to defraud, confuse, injure, or obtain personal information — § 501.059(8)(b)–(c).
  - Private action — partially verified: § 501.059(10)(a) provides that an aggrieved called party may bring an action to enjoin a violation; the remainder of (10) (damages/fees) was not opened successfully in this run.
- **Debt collection — Ch. 559, Part VI, Florida Consumer Collection Practices Act (FCCPA):**
  - Part VI contents (2024): §§ 559.552, 559.553, 559.554, 559.5541, 559.555, 559.5558, 559.565, 559.715, 559.72, 559.725, 559.730, et seq.
  - State + federal law: Nothing in Part VI limits the federal Fair Debt Collection Practices Act; Part VI is in addition to it, and where they conflict, the provision more protective of the consumer/debtor prevails — § 559.552.
  - Prohibited practices include: simulating a law-enforcement officer or government representative; using/threatening force or violence; contacting a debtor's employer before final judgment without written permission/acknowledgment; disclosing a disputed debt without disclosing the dispute; harassing frequency/conduct; profane/obscene/vulgar/willfully abusive language; claiming a debt/right the collector knows is not legitimate/existent; simulating legal/judicial process; communicating under the guise of an attorney; publishing a "deadbeat list"; refusing adequate identification when requested; embarrassing markings on the outside of mail; and contacting the debtor 9 p.m.–8 a.m. in the debtor's time zone without prior consent (email excepted if otherwise compliant) — § 559.72(1)–(19).
  - Attorney-represented debtor: A collector may not communicate with a debtor known to be represented by an attorney on that debt (if the attorney's name/address is known or readily ascertainable), unless the attorney fails to respond within 30 days, consents, or the debtor initiates contact — § 559.72(18).
  - Civil remedies — not fully verified: § 559.77 is listed in Part VI as the civil-remedies section, but its current text/damages were not opened successfully in this run.
  - AG complaint route for debt issues: Complaints against a credit counselor, debt-management firm, or debt collector may be filed with the Attorney General online at myfloridalegal.com or by phone at 1-866-9-NO-SCAM, and also with the Florida Office of Financial Regulation and the CFPB.

## 32. Employment law

- **At-will employment:** No Florida statute codifies at-will employment; it is a common-law default, and no section in ch. 448 states it. The statutes below are the statutory limits on it (discrimination, whistleblower, and minimum-wage retaliation bans).
- **Wage payment (ch. 448):**
  - The Florida Minimum Wage Act is the wage-enforcement core of ch. 448: an aggrieved person must give written pre-suit notice identifying the wage claimed, work dates/hours, and total unpaid wages, and the employer has 15 calendar days to pay or resolve the claim; the limitations period is tolled during those 15 days. — § 448.110(6)(a)–(b)
  - Prevailing minimum-wage claimants recover full back wages plus the same amount as liquidated damages, plus reasonable attorney's fees and costs (reduced only on a proven good-faith defense). — § 448.110(6)(c)
  - Day laborers paid through a labor pool must be paid by cash, negotiable instrument, payroll debit card, or electronic fund transfer; the pool must give notice of the payment method before the first pay period and an itemized statement of deductions at each payment. — § 448.24(2)
- **Florida minimum wage (Art. X, § 24, Fla. Const.):** Minimum wage rose $10.00/hr on Sept. 30, 2021, then $1.00 each Sept. 30 until $15.00/hr on Sept. 30, 2026; from Sept. 30, 2027 it is adjusted annually for inflation (CPI-W), effective each Jan. 1. For tipped employees, employers may credit tips up to the 2003 FLSA tip-credit amount. — Art. X, § 24(c). Retaliation for exercising rights under the amendment is unlawful, and aggrieved persons may sue for back wages plus equal liquidated damages, fees, costs, reinstatement, and injunctive relief. — Art. X, § 24(d)–(e). § 448.110 implements § 24: the Department of Commerce calculates the annual inflation adjustment and publishes it by Oct. 15 each year. — § 448.110(2), (4)
- **Discrimination (ch. 760, Florida Civil Rights Act):**
  - The Act's purpose is freedom from discrimination because of race, color, religion, sex, pregnancy, national origin, age, handicap, or marital status. — § 760.01(2)
  - It is an unlawful employment practice for an employer to discharge or refuse to hire, or otherwise discriminate in compensation, terms, conditions, or privileges of employment, because of race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, handicap, or marital status. — § 760.10(1)(a)
  - Remedies run through the Florida Commission on Human Relations (created by § 760.03) under § 760.11, and any violation of a Florida anti-discrimination statute gives rise to a cause of action for the relief and damages in § 760.11(5). — §§ 760.07, 760.11
- **Noncompete agreements (§ 542.335):**
  - Restrictive covenants are enforceable if reasonable in time, area, and line of business; a court may not enforce one unless it is in a writing signed by the person against whom enforcement is sought. — § 542.335(1), (1)(a)
  - The enforcing party must plead and prove a legitimate business interest (trade secrets, valuable confidential information, substantial customer relationships, customer goodwill, or extraordinary/specialized training); a covenant without one is unlawful, void, and unenforceable. — § 542.335(1)(b)
  - Time presumptions for a former employee (non-trade-secret, non-sale): 6 months or less is presumed reasonable; more than 2 years is presumed unreasonable. For trade-secret covenants: 5 years or less presumed reasonable, more than 10 years presumed unreasonable. — § 542.335(1)(d)–(e)
- **Whistleblower protection (§ 448.102, private sector):**
  - An employer may not take retaliatory personnel action because the employee (1) disclosed or threatened to disclose, under oath and in writing to an appropriate governmental agency, an employer activity/policy/practice violating a law, rule, or regulation; (2) provided information or testified in a government investigation, hearing, or inquiry; or (3) objected to or refused to participate in a violating activity, policy, or practice. — § 448.102(1)–(3)
  - For a (1) disclosure, the employee must first have brought the matter to a supervisor or the employer in writing and afforded a reasonable opportunity to correct it. — § 448.102(1)
  - Remedy: civil action within 2 years after discovering the retaliatory action or 4 years after it was taken, whichever is earlier. — § 448.103(1)(a)
- **Unemployment compensation (ch. 443, "reemployment assistance"):**
  - Eligibility requires, among other things, a claim for the week, work registration, being able to work and available for work, actively seeking work including contacting at least five prospective employers per week, and a 1-week waiting period. — § 443.091(1)
  - To establish a benefit year: wage credits in two or more base-period quarters, and total base-period wages of at least 1.5× high-quarter wages and at least $3,400. — § 443.111(2)
  - Weekly benefit amount is 1/26 of high-quarter wages, not less than $32 or more than $275. — § 443.111(3)
  - Duration is 12 weeks if the state average unemployment rate is at or below 5%, plus one week per 0.5% above 5%, up to 23 weeks at 10.5% or higher; total benefits capped at 25% of base-period wages or $6,325, whichever is less. — § 443.111(5)

  - A license application must be approved or denied within 90 days after receipt of a completed application (unless a shorter period applies by law), tolled by a §§ 120.569/120.57 proceeding; an application not acted on within the applicable period (90 days, 15 days after a public hearing, or 45 days after a recommended order, whichever is latest) is considered approved unless the recommended order recommends denial. — § 120.60(1)

  - If immediate serious danger to public health, safety, or welfare requires it, the agency may order emergency suspension, restriction, or limitation by any procedure fair under the circumstances, but must also promptly institute and act on a §§ 120.569/120.57 suspension/revocation proceeding. — § 120.60(6)
- **Declaratory statements — § 120.565:**
  - Any substantially affected person may seek a declaratory statement of the agency's opinion on the applicability of a statutory provision, or of any rule or order of the agency, as it applies to the petitioner's particular set of circumstances. — § 120.565(1)

## 35. Constitutional law and civil rights

- **Florida Constitution, Art. I — Declaration of Rights:**
  - Art. I, § 2 — Basic rights: "All natural persons are equal before the law and have inalienable rights, among which are the right to enjoy and defend life and liberty, to pursue happiness, to be rewarded for industry, and to acquire, possess and protect property." It also bars deprivation of any right because of race, religion, or physical handicap (1968 text as reproduced; later amendments updated this list — verify current wording).
  - Art. I, § 9 — Due process: "No person shall be deprived of life, liberty or property without due process of law." The section also contains the double-jeopardy and self-incrimination protections (full text not re-opened in this run due to the 429).
  - Art. I, § 12 — Search and seizure: "The right of the people to be secure in their persons, houses, papers and effects against unreasonable searches and seizures, and against the unreasonable interception of private communications by any means, shall not be violated. No warrant shall be issued except upon probable cause, supported by affidavit, particularly describing the place or places to be searched, the person or persons, thing or things to be seized, the communication to be intercepted, and the nature of evidence to be obtained."
  - Art. I, § 12 — Conformity clause (1982 amendment): "This right shall be construed in conformity with the 4th Amendment to the United States Constitution, as interpreted by the United States Supreme Court. Articles or information obtained in violation of this right shall not be admissible in evidence if such articles or information would be inadmissible under decisions of the United States Supreme Court construing the 4th Amendment."
  - Art. I, § 16 — Rights of accused; victims: § 16(a) sets out the rights of the accused; § 16(b) gives crime victims "the right to be informed, to be present, and to be heard when relevant, at all crucial stages of criminal proceedings, to the extent that these rights do not interfere with the constitutional rights of the accused."
  - Art. I, § 23 — Right of privacy: "Every natural person has the right to be let alone and free from governmental intrusion into the person's private life except as otherwise provided herein. This section shall not be construed to limit the public's right of access to public records and meetings as provided by law." Added 1980.
  - Art. I, § 24 — Access to public records and meetings: (a) "Every person has the right to inspect or copy any public record made or received in connection with the official business of any public body, officer, or employee of the state, or persons acting on their behalf," covering legislative, executive, and judicial branches, counties, municipalities, districts, and constitutional officers, except records exempted under the section or made confidential by the Constitution. (b) Meetings of any collegial public body where official acts are taken or public business is transacted/discussed "shall be open and noticed to the public." (c) Self-executing; exemptions require a general law passed by two-thirds vote of each house, stating the public necessity and no broader than necessary.
- **Public records — Ch. 119:**
  - § 119.01(1): "It is the policy of this state that all state, county, and municipal records are open for personal inspection and copying by any person. Providing access to public records is a duty of each agency."
  - § 119.011(12): "Public records" means all documents, papers, letters, maps, books, tapes, photographs, films, sound recordings, data processing software, or other material, regardless of physical form or means of transmission, made or received pursuant to law/ordinance or in connection with official business by any agency.
  - § 119.07: Custodians must permit inspection/copying at any reasonable time, under reasonable conditions, under custodian supervision; sets fees (including up to 15 cents per one-sided copy) and exemptions. § 119.10: Violation penalties. § 119.11: Accelerated hearing; immediate compliance.
- **Open meetings — Ch. 286 (Sunshine Law):**
  - § 286.011(1): "All meetings of any board or commission of any state agency or authority or of any agency or authority of any county, municipal corporation, or political subdivision… at which official acts are to be taken are declared to be public meetings open to the public at all times, and no resolution, rule, or formal action shall be considered binding except as taken or made at such meeting," with reasonable notice required.
  - § 286.011(2): Minutes "shall be promptly recorded, and such records shall be open to public inspection."
  - § 286.011(3): Violation by a public officer is a noncriminal infraction, fine up to $500; a member who knowingly violates it by attending a non-conforming meeting is guilty of a second-degree misdemeanor. § 286.011(6): No meeting at a facility that discriminates on the basis of sex, age, race, creed, color, origin, or economic status, or that unreasonably restricts public access. § 286.0114: Reasonable opportunity to be heard at public meetings.
- **Florida Civil Rights Act — Ch. 760:**
  - § 760.01(2): The Florida Civil Rights Act of 1992 (ss. 760.01–760.11 and 509.092) aims "to secure for all individuals within the state freedom from discrimination because of race, color, religion, sex, pregnancy, national origin, age, handicap, or marital status and thereby to protect their interest in personal dignity."
  - § 760.05–760.06: Functions and powers of the Florida Commission on Human Relations. § 760.07: Remedies for unlawful discrimination. § 760.08: Discrimination in places of public accommodation prohibited.
  - § 760.10: Unlawful employment practices (e.g., discharge, refusal to hire, or discrimination in compensation/terms/conditions). § 760.11: Administrative and civil remedies; construction.
  - Ch. 760, Part II — Florida Fair Housing Act: Equal access to fair housing, prohibiting discrimination in sale/rental and related brokerage/financial services based on race, color, national origin, sex, handicap, familial status, or religion.
- **Florida vs. federal rights — key differences:**
  - Privacy (§ 23): Florida has an express textual right "to be let alone"; the U.S. Constitution has no express privacy clause — Florida's protection is broader in scope than the federal implied right.
  - Search/seizure (§ 12): Unlike most state constitutions, Florida's conformity clause *caps* protection at the federal Fourth Amendment as interpreted by the U.S. Supreme Court — Florida courts "provide no greater protection than those interpretations."
  - Open government (§ 24, chs. 119/286): Florida's access right is constitutional, self-executing, and exemption-proof except by two-thirds legislative vote; there is no federal constitutional equivalent.
  - General rule: Where the Florida text has no federal counterpart (§ 23, § 24) or no conformity clause, Florida courts may read it more protectively than federal law; where it has a conformity clause (§ 12), they may not.

## 36. Evidence — Florida Evidence Code, Ch. 90

- **Scope:** Ch. 90 is the "Florida Evidence Code." — § 90.101
- **Relevance — §§ 90.401–90.403:**
  - § 90.401: "Relevant evidence is evidence tending to prove or disprove a material fact."
  - § 90.402: "All relevant evidence is admissible, except as provided by law."
  - § 90.403: Relevant evidence is inadmissible if its probative value is substantially outweighed by danger of unfair prejudice, confusion of issues, misleading the jury, or needless presentation of cumulative evidence; the section is not to be construed to make evidence of available third-party benefits inadmissible.
- **Character evidence — § 90.404:**
  - § 90.404(1): Evidence of a person's character or trait is inadmissible to prove action in conformity with it on a particular occasion, except: (a) pertinent trait of the accused, offered by the accused or by the prosecution to rebut it; (b) pertinent trait of the victim, offered by the accused or by the prosecution to rebut it, and in a homicide case the victim's peacefulness to rebut evidence the victim was the aggressor (subject to § 794.022); (c) character of a witness, as provided in §§ 90.608–90.610.
  - § 90.404(2)(a): Similar-fact evidence of other crimes, wrongs, or acts is admissible when relevant to prove a material fact in issue, such as motive, opportunity, intent, preparation, plan, knowledge, identity, or absence of mistake or accident, but inadmissible when relevant solely to prove bad character or propensity.
  - § 90.404(2)(b)–(c): In child-molestation and sexual-offense cases, evidence of the defendant's commission of other such crimes, wrongs, or acts is admissible and may be considered for its bearing on any matter to which it is relevant.
  - § 90.404(2)(d): When the state intends to offer such other-offense evidence in a criminal action, it must furnish the accused a written statement of the acts/offenses at least 10 days before trial, with the particularity required of an indictment or information; on request, the court charges the jury on the limited purpose.
- **Hearsay — §§ 90.801–90.804:**
  - § 90.801(1): A "statement" is an oral or written assertion, or nonverbal conduct intended as an assertion; a "declarant" is the person who makes it; "hearsay" is a statement, other than one made by the declarant while testifying at the trial or hearing, offered to prove the truth of the matter asserted.
  - § 90.801(2): A statement is not hearsay if the declarant testifies and is subject to cross-examination and the statement is (a) inconsistent with testimony and given under oath subject to perjury at a trial, hearing, other proceeding, or deposition; (b) consistent with testimony and offered to rebut a charge of improper influence, motive, or recent fabrication; or (c) an identification of a person made after perceiving the person.
  - § 90.802: "Except as provided by statute, hearsay evidence is inadmissible."
  - § 90.803: Exceptions where availability of the declarant is immaterial, including (1) spontaneous statement, (2) excited utterance, (3) then-existing mental, emotional, or physical condition, (4) statements for medical diagnosis or treatment, (5) recorded recollection, (6) records of regularly conducted business activity, (18) admissions, (22) former testimony, and (23) statement of a child victim.
  - § 90.804(1): "Unavailability" means the declarant is (a) exempted by court ruling on privilege grounds, (b) persists in refusing to testify despite a court order, (c) lacks memory so as to destroy effectiveness as a witness, (d) unable to be present/testify because of death or then-existing physical or mental illness or infirmity, or (e) absent despite process or other reasonable means; a declarant is not unavailable if that condition is due to the proponent's procurement or wrongdoing.
  - § 90.804(2): If the declarant is unavailable, exceptions include (a) former testimony, (b) statement under belief of impending death, (c) statement against interest, and (d) statement of personal or family history.
- **Privileges — §§ 90.501–90.506:**
  - § 90.501: Except as otherwise provided by Ch. 90, any other statute, or the U.S. or Florida Constitution, no person in a legal proceeding has a privilege to refuse to be a witness, refuse to disclose any matter, refuse to produce any object or writing, or prevent another from doing so.
  - §§ 90.5015–90.506, as listed in Ch. 90: 90.5015 journalist's privilege; 90.502 lawyer-client privilege; 90.5021 fiduciary lawyer-client privilege; 90.503 psychotherapist-patient privilege; 90.5035 sexual assault counselor-victim privilege; 90.5036 domestic violence advocate-victim privilege; 90.504 husband-wife privilege; 90.505 privilege as to communications to clergy; 90.5055 accountant-client privilege; 90.506 trade secrets.
  - § 90.502: A client has a privilege to refuse to disclose, and to prevent any other person from disclosing, confidential communications made in the rendition of legal services.
  - § 90.503: A patient has a privilege to refuse to disclose, and to prevent any other person from disclosing, confidential communications or records made for diagnosis or treatment of a mental or emotional condition, including alcoholism and other drug addiction.
  - § 90.506: A person has a privilege to refuse to disclose, and to prevent others from disclosing, a trade secret owned by that person if allowing the privilege will not conceal fraud or otherwise work injustice; when the court directs disclosure, it must take protective measures required by the holder's interests, the parties' interests, and the furtherance of justice.
- **Authentication — § 90.901:** "Authentication or identification of evidence is required as a condition precedent to its admissibility. The requirements of this section are satisfied by evidence sufficient to support a finding that the matter in question is what its proponent claims."
- **Expert testimony — § 90.702, Daubert:** If scientific, technical, or other specialized knowledge will assist the trier of fact in understanding the evidence or determining a fact in issue, a witness qualified as an expert by knowledge, skill, experience, training, or education may testify in the form of an opinion or otherwise, if: (1) the testimony is based upon sufficient facts or data; (2) it is the product of reliable principles and methods; and (3) the witness has applied the principles and methods reliably to the facts of the case. That amendment is the legislative adoption of the Daubert / Federal Rule 702 framework in Florida.

## 37. Appellate procedure — Fla. R. App. P.

- **Primary source for the Rules:** Florida Rules of Appellate Procedure (June 1, 2026), https://www-media.floridabar.org/uploads/2026/06/Appellate-Court-Rules-06-01-26.pdf
- **Notice of appeal — final orders:**
  - **30 days, jurisdictional:** "Jurisdiction of the court under this rule must be invoked by filing a notice with the clerk of the lower tribunal within 30 days of rendition of the order to be reviewed" — Fla. R. App. P. 9.110(b).
  - **Late = no jurisdiction:** "Where the notice of appeal is not filed within thirty days of rendition, the appellate court is precluded from exercising jurisdiction" — Fla. R. App. P. 9.110(b), quoted in 4th DCA opinion.
  - **Rendition / tolling:** An order is rendered when a signed, written order is filed with the clerk of the lower tribunal, Fla. R. App. P. 9.020(h); a timely authorized motion for rehearing tolls rendition / the 30 days — Fla. R. App. P. 9.020(h)(1)-(2), 9.110(b).
  - **Cross-appeal:** Appellee may cross-appeal by serving notice within 10 days of service of appellant's timely notice or within the Rule 9.110(b) period, whichever is later — Fla. R. App. P. 9.110(g).
- **Record on appeal:**
  - Contents: Except as otherwise designated, the record is all documents filed in the lower tribunal, all exhibits that are not physical evidence, and any transcript(s) filed there, except summonses, praecipes, subpoenas, returns, notices of hearing/deposition, depositions, and other discovery; it also includes a progress docket — Fla. R. App. P. 9.200(a)(1).
  - Directions: Appellant may direct the clerk to include/exclude documents within 10 days of filing the notice of appeal; appellee may direct additional documents within 20 days — Fla. R. App. P. 9.200(a)(2).
  - Transcripts: Appellant designates portions for transcription within 10 days of the notice; appellee may designate more within 20 days; reporter must transcribe/file within 30 days of designation — Fla. R. App. P. 9.200(b)(1), (b)(3).
  - No transcript: Parties may use a stipulated statement showing how issues arose and were decided, with the order reviewed and necessary record attached — Fla. R. App. P. 9.200(a)(4) [now (a)(3) in current numbering; verify against the 2026 PDF before citing the sub-number].
- **Interlocutory (nonfinal) appeals — Rule 9.130:**
  - Limited categories only; narrowly construed: Appeals to the DCAs of nonfinal orders are limited to those that (A) concern venue; (B) grant/continue/modify/deny/dissolve injunctions; (C) determine jurisdiction of the person, right to immediate possession of property, family-law immediate monetary relief / custody-time-sharing / invalid marital agreement, insurance-appraisal entitlement, workers' compensation immunity, class certification, and other listed matters — Fla. R. App. P. 9.130(a)(3).
  - 30 days: Jurisdiction under 9.130(a)(3)-(a)(5) is invoked by filing a notice with the clerk of the lower tribunal within 30 days of rendition; notice is designated "notice of appeal of nonfinal order," per Rule 9.900(c) — Fla. R. App. P. 9.130(b)-(c).
  - Scope: Rule 9.130(a)(1) applies to appeals to the DCAs of the nonfinal orders authorized therein; criminal nonfinal orders are under Rule 9.140, and other nonfinal orders are reviewed by the Rule 9.100 method — Fla. R. App. P. 9.130(a)(1)-(2).
- **Certiorari / original proceedings — Rule 9.100:**
  - Writs covered: Rule 9.100 applies to proceedings invoking jurisdiction under Rule 9.030(a)(3), (b)(2)-(3), (c)(2)-(3) for writs of mandamus, prohibition, quo warranto, certiorari, and habeas corpus, and all writs necessary to the complete exercise of the courts' jurisdiction; and for review of nonfinal administrative action — Fla. R. App. P. 9.100(a).
  - How invoked / deadline: Original jurisdiction is invoked by filing a petition with the clerk of the court deemed to have jurisdiction (not a notice in the lower tribunal); a petition for certiorari, for review of quasi-judicial local-government action, nonfinal agency action, or prisoner disciplinary action must be filed within 30 days of rendition — Fla. R. App. P. 9.100(b)-(c).
  - Threshold for relief: Court may issue an order to show cause if the petition shows a preliminary basis for relief, a departure from the essential requirements of law causing material injury for which there is no adequate remedy by appeal, or that review of final administrative action would not provide an adequate remedy — Fla. R. App. P. 9.100(h).
- **Which court hears what:**
  - Four levels: "The judicial power shall be vested in a supreme court, district courts of appeal, circuit courts and county courts" — Fla. Const. art. V, § 1.
  - Florida Supreme Court, mandatory: "Shall hear appeals from final judgments of trial courts imposing the death penalty and from decisions of district courts of appeal declaring invalid a state statute or a provision of the state constitution" — Fla. Const. art. V, § 3(b)(1).
  - Supreme Court, when provided by general law: Bond/certificate-of-indebtedness validation appeals, and review of statewide utility rate/service action — Fla. Const. art. V, § 3(b)(2).
  - Supreme Court, discretionary: May review a DCA decision that expressly declares valid a state statute, expressly construes a state or federal constitutional provision, expressly affects a class of constitutional or state officers, or expressly and directly conflicts with another DCA or Supreme Court decision on the same question of law — Fla. Const. art. V, § 3(b)(3).
  - DCAs: "Shall have jurisdiction to hear appeals, that may be taken as a matter of right, from final judgments or orders of trial courts, including those entered on review of administrative action, not directly appealable to the supreme court or a circuit court. They may review interlocutory orders in such cases to the extent provided by rules adopted by the supreme court" — Fla. Const. art. V, § 4(b)(1); mirrored in Fla. R. App. P. 9.030(b)(1)(A) (final orders) and 9.030(b)(1)(B) (nonfinal orders as prescribed by Rule 9.130).
  - Circuit courts (county-court appeals): "The circuit courts shall have original jurisdiction not vested in the county courts, and jurisdiction of appeals when provided by general law" — Fla. Const. art. V, § 5(b); they may also issue mandamus, quo warranto, certiorari, prohibition, and habeas corpus. Gap: the county-to-circuit exceptions (county order declaring a statute/constitutional provision invalid; county order certified as of great public importance and accepted by the DCA) are in general law (§ 26.012, Fla. ## 38. Local government

- **Counties — Ch. 125:**
  - § 125.01(1): The board of county commissioners is the county's legislative and governing body, with power to carry on county government "to the extent not inconsistent with general or special law."
  - § 125.01(1)(g)-(h), (q)-(r), (t), (w): Powers include preparing/enforcing comprehensive plans, establishing/enforcing zoning and business regulations, creating municipal service taxing/benefit units in unincorporated areas, levying/collecting taxes and special assessments, adopting ordinances/resolutions and prescribing fines/penalties, and exercising all powers "not specifically prohibited by law."

  - §§ 125.80-125.88, Part IV: Optional county charters.
- **Municipalities — Ch. 166:**
  - § 166.021(1): Under Art. VIII, § 2(b), Fla. Const., municipalities have governmental, corporate, and proprietary powers and "may exercise any power for municipal purposes, except when expressly prohibited by law."
  - § 166.021(3): A municipality may legislate on any subject the Legislature may act on, except annexation/merger/extraterritorial power, subjects expressly prohibited by the constitution, subjects expressly preempted to state/county government, and subjects preempted to a county by county charter.
  - § 166.021(4): The section is to be construed "to secure for municipalities the broad exercise of home rule powers granted by the constitution."
  - § 166.041: Ordinance = regulation of a general and permanent nature enforceable as local law; resolution = administrative/temporary expression. A proposed ordinance is read on at least 2 separate days and noticed at least 10 days before adoption in a newspaper of general circulation; an emergency ordinance may be enacted by two-thirds vote.
- **Comprehensive Planning / Growth Management — Ch. 163:**
  - § 163.3161, Community Planning Act: Purpose is to utilize/strengthen local governments' role in comprehensive planning to guide/manage future development; the state role is focused on protecting important state resources/facilities; no development is permitted except in conformity with comprehensive plans.
  - § 163.3167(1)-(2): Municipalities and counties have power/responsibility to plan for future development/growth, adopt/amend comprehensive plans, implement them by land development regulations, and maintain administrative instruments/procedures; each local government shall maintain a comprehensive plan as required by Part II.
  - § 163.3167(3): A municipality incorporated after the act's effective date must establish a local planning agency within 1 year and adopt a comprehensive plan within 3 years; the county plan controls until then.
  - § 163.3177: The comprehensive plan must consist of principles/guidelines/standards for orderly, balanced future development; its elements must be consistent and the plan financially feasible, including a capital improvements element covering at least 5 years.
- **Code Enforcement — Ch. 162:**
  - § 162.01-162.02, Part I: §§ 162.01-162.13 are the "Local Government Code Enforcement Boards Act"; intent is to promote/protect/improve health, safety, and welfare by authorizing administrative boards to impose administrative fines and other noncriminal penalties as an equitable, expeditious, effective, inexpensive enforcement method.
  - § 162.03, § 162.05-162.09: Each county/municipality may, at its option, create or abolish code enforcement boards by ordinance; boards are organized under § 162.05, hear cases under §§ 162.06-162.07, exercise powers under § 162.08, and impose administrative fines, repair costs, and liens under § 162.09 (lien duration § 162.10, appeals § 162.11).
  - §§ 162.21-162.30, Part II: Supplemental county/municipal code/ordinance enforcement procedures, including § 162.21 enforcement/penalties, § 162.22 designation of enforcement methods/penalties for municipal ordinances, and § 162.30 civil actions to enforce county/municipal ordinances.
- **Special Districts — Ch. 189:**
  - § 189.011(1): Intent is to provide general provisions for definition, creation, and operation of special districts; dependent special districts are created at the prerogative of counties/municipalities, and independent special districts only by legislative authorization.
  - § 189.011(2)-(3): Special districts serve a necessary/useful public purpose, secured by minimum accountability standards requiring registration and reporting of financial/other activities; districts must cooperate/coordinate with the general-purpose local governments where located.
  - § 189.4155: Construction/expansion/major alteration of a public facility by a special district must be consistent with the applicable local government comprehensive plan adopted under Ch. 163, Part II, subject to the stated limits on requiring a district to build.
- **Home Rule — Art. VIII, Fla. Const.:**
  - Art. VIII, § 1(f): Non-charter counties have such power of self-government as provided by general or special law. § 1(g): Charter counties have all powers of local self-government not inconsistent with general law, or with special law approved by vote of the electors.
  - Art. VIII, § 1(c)-(e): A county government may be established by charter adopted/amended/repealed only on vote of county electors; each county elects a sheriff, tax collector, property appraiser, supervisor of elections, and clerk of the circuit court for 4-year terms; the governing body is otherwise a board of 5 or 7 county commissioners serving staggered 4-year terms.
  - Art. VIII, § 2(a)-(b): Municipalities may be established/abolished and charters amended under general or special law; municipalities have governmental, corporate, and proprietary powers to conduct municipal government, perform municipal functions, and render municipal services, and may exercise any power for municipal purposes "except as otherwise provided by law."
